# Unknown Language on the show

How this game was played on The Devil's Plan. Our version: [rules page](../games/unknown-language.md).
Recorded from the NamuWiki episode guides (machine-translated), in our own words. Contestants
are Player A, B …; letters are assigned separately in each section. **Gaps** lists what the source
leaves open. Index: [On the show](README.md).

## Season 3 (Dead Again), Exile (Days 1 to 4): hidden stage, the language of exile
- **Name in the source:** the language of exile, in the "place of exile" (a hidden stage)
- **Players:** eliminated players living in the exile place, at most 4; they worked on it together, with newcomers joining over the days
- **Stakes:** reading the carvings reveals the rules of exile and how to open the trial that leads back into the game

### Rules on the show
- An eliminated player first chose in a room of choice between two doors: blue ends their journey, red leads to exile with another chance to return. Water, food, mattresses (4) and hourglasses were in the place.
- The floor carries pictures and many glyphs in 15 areas set in a circle (the numbering is the wiki's). Nothing is explained in advance.
- **Words:** a root, mostly a pictograph, with a part-of-speech mark above it: a level bar = noun, a dot = verb, a rising stroke = adjective, a falling stroke = adverb. Words without a mark are mainly function words: conjunctions, "with"-type prepositions, "can", plural markers, numerals, equals and inequality signs. Two dots under a pictograph make the colour of that object.
- A mark can turn one root into a related word (for example "live" as a verb and "house" as a noun; "head" turned into a verb for "know").
- Sentence order: subject, predicate, object or complement. The same verb can mean "die" with only a subject and "kill" when there is an object.
- A dot at the end of a numeral's stroke makes it an ordinal.
- Food and water spots are labelled in the script; three hourglasses are labelled 1 hour, 15 minutes and 1 minute.
- **The 15 areas:**
  1. Short sentences with "there is": a person on a road, a tree at the road's end.
  2. Floor tiles with Korean on the back; rearranged they give the rules: this place is neither life nor death; at most four may stay; with four, one or two die in a match; there are two ways to live: pass God's test, or win the last match on the sixth day.
  3. Simple facts about animals: who can make sounds and who cannot, who eats what, a bird building a nest in a tree, numbers of arms, legs, head and tail, an egg becoming a small bird.
  4. The trial: a grid with the number 1 already set; the sums across, down and diagonally are equal; make it to pass, fail and you die; once one person has passed, the trial is over.
  5. A person looking at the scorpion constellation; a shadow under a person.
  6. "Sums" of things and colours: wood and fire make charcoal, red and blue make purple, sand and fire make glass, sea minus water is salt, purple with the transparent colour stays purple, and a line of digit arithmetic.
  7. The entrance door (also used to reach the toilet below).
  8. The numerals drawn as a clockwise spiral.
  9. A sentence about a small person killing a big person through a window.
  10. A colour chart: red (fire), blue (sea), white (salt), purple (object unclear); transparent and black appear elsewhere.
  11. Poison: a snake and a scorpion's tail have it, a person who eats it dies; plus a short message about living and sand.
  12. How to open the way: the god of the black hole watches you; the black moon is below this place and is found by following where the dead look; at the sixth bird cry, cover the sun; when day turns to night the road appears; the black hole lies at its end and holds God's test.
  13. A black bird eating a dead person; the dead cannot make sounds.
  14. Time units: 60 minutes an hour, 24 hours a day, one turn of the earth a day, one circuit of the sun 365 days.
  15. The head has 7 openings: the big one eats or speaks, the upper two see, those at left and right hear.
- **Opening the trial:** 30 minutes after sunrise, on the sixth bird cry, cover the sun's "eye" with the black moon, using a pole assembled for the purpose.

### How it went
- Day 1: Player A and Player B, first to arrive, quickly read the food and water labels, used the clock and inferred a capacity of 4 from the mattresses. B worked out parts of speech from areas 1 and 3 and read most of areas 9 and 5; area 15 resisted. After the prison match Player C and Player D arrived; D found at once that the tiles of area 2 have Korean on the back. Readings in areas 3 and 9 were corrected and area 15 was finished.
- Day 2: B at first refused to share, treating it as a solo contest, then shared, once C had shared a reading and A had been friendly to him: the two dots mark colours, and areas 10 and 6 deal with them. A newcomer, Player E, found the black moon under the white disc of area 10 by intuition, before the text pointing to it was solved. Two later arrivals, Player F and Player G, were taught by C. F proposed laying out area 2 with marks and roots in alternating rows, reading in Korean and in the script at once. G found the stick for the moon.
- Day 3: they decided to raise the moon at the sixth cry, using an hourglass to time it. Area 4 was nearly read and taken to be a magic square. Another newcomer, Player H, was taught the script and learned it fast.
- Day 4: with all of it deciphered, they found the birds cry at 8:30, and Player F covered the sun on the sixth cry. It went dark, a glowing floor came and carried F to the trial.

### Result
All areas deciphered by Day 4; the way to the trial was opened (see `magic-square`).

### Differences from our version
- Ours is a solo quiz of 9 multiple-choice questions in 3 stages, scored 100 each; the show's was an open-ended, cooperative decoding of a room over several days.
- Ours generates glyphs and the meaning of the marks from a seed; the show had one fixed script.
- Ours has three parts of speech (bar, dot, ring); the show had four marks (bar, dot, rising and falling strokes) plus unmarked function words, colour dots, numerals and ordinals.
- Ours varies the word order and the adjective position; the show's order was subject, predicate, object or complement.
- Ours has no hidden physical tiles, trial or return to the game.

### Gaps
- The glyphs themselves are pictures and were not readable as text.
- Several area translations are rough after machine translation (area 11's message, the arithmetic line in area 6, the purple object in area 10, which areas relate adjectives to adverbs).
