## Season 2 (Death Room), Day 5: prison match
- **Name in the source:** Wall Go (also rendered "Ball Go" or "wall Baduk")
- **Players:** the 3 prisoners; round 1 with all three, round 2 a duel between the two who did not win round 1.
- **Stakes:** the round 1 winner was safe and got 1 piece; the loser of round 2 was eliminated.

### Rules on the show
- **Board:** 7 x 7 squares (columns A to G, rows 1 to 7). Walls stand on the sides between squares.
- **Round 1 setup:** turn order by lot. Each player places 2 markers, in snake order 1-2-3-3-2-1.
- **Turn** (90 seconds): move one of your markers, then build a wall on one side (top, bottom, left or right) of the square where that marker ends.
- **Movement:** 1 or 2 squares orthogonally, turns allowed. The move log also shows markers ending on their starting square. A marker may not enter a square holding another marker and may not pass a wall.
- A wall cannot be built where one already stands.
- **Timeout:** if the turn is not finished in 90 seconds, a wall goes up on a random side.
- **Wall break:** once per game each player may break through a wall during the movement step.
- **End:** immediately when walls separate all players' territories from each other, even if some wall breaks are unused. Until then every player has to keep taking turns, also inside their own closed land.
- **Result:** the largest territory (square count) wins.
- **Round 2 changes:** 4 markers each, of which 2 start on fixed squares and 2 are placed by the players (placement order 1-2-2-1); no wall break. The other rules are the same. The player with more territory in round 1 appears to move first.

### How it went
**Round 1** (turn order A, B, C)
- Placement: A on C3 and D4, B on E5 and B5, C on E3 and C5.
- A spent most turns pressing and boxing in C with markers and walls, and checked B very little. B grew from the corner and the left side.
- C pushed toward the upper right and later tried to expand from the middle.
- Around move 20, B deliberately left a narrow piece of own land open: closing it early would have forced B to keep shrinking it with later walls.
- Moves 25 to 27: A used a marker to block C's path; B sealed off the left and lower left, which C could no longer contest.
- Move 28: A tried the wall break under time pressure, ran out of time and got a random wall.
- Move 29: B closed the game with one wall, after hesitating because it would send the other two into a duel; C urged B to finish.

**Round 2** (C moved first)
- Placed markers: C on E4 and D5, A on B4 and D4. From the move log the fixed markers were C on B6 and F2, A on B2 and F6.
- The early fight was in the upper middle and right; A kept C's markers in check along the centre for a while.
- C admitted a weak move 19, but A answered on move 20 with a worse one that left an own marker open to being trapped, which C did on move 21.
- A then built a wall that hindered A's own path, and in the last fight on the left was short of time (move 26).
- Move 29: C ended the game.

### Result
- Round 1: B 23 squares, C 16, A 10. B survived and received 1 piece.
- Round 2: C 27 squares, A 22. C survived; A was eliminated.

### Differences from our version
- Show: players place their markers in snake order (round 2: two placed, two fixed). Ours: fixed start squares dealt by the seed.
- Show: two rounds, 3 players then a duel with 4 markers each. Ours: one game of 3 players with 2 pieces each.
- Show: one wall break per player in round 1. Ours: walls never come down.
- Show: 90-second turn limit with a random wall on timeout. Ours: no timer.
- Show: all players keep moving until the game ends. Ours: a sealed-in player skips turns.
- Show: largest territory wins. Ours: the same, scored as 100 + 20 per square.

### Gaps
- The wall-break rule is garbled: it is clear that it is used once, in the movement step, but not exactly how far the marker may travel or whether the broken wall disappears.
- The guide lists movement as 1 or 2 squares; whether "stay" is a free choice or only a move out and back is unclear.
- Whether the board edge can take a wall is not said.
- Tie-breaks for equal territory are not given.
- The sentence on the first-player rule for round 2 is garbled; "larger territory moves first" is an inference that matches the broadcast order.
- A four-player version evidently exists (seen in practice play) but was not explained.

## Season 2 (Death Room), game-rules page
Source: the game-rules page (board games in the wings) and the folded and rule text of the Day 5 page. The general Wall Go page linked from the wiki has no rules of its own; it refers to the day pages.

### Rules added
- Wall Go lay in both the living building and the prison wing as a board game for two or four players, before it appeared as the fifth prison match.
- A marker's move may bring it back to the square it started from.
- Building a wall is compulsory on every turn, on one of the four sides of the moved marker's square, where no wall stands yet.
- Wall break: in the one logged use (round 1, move 28), the marker went one square through a wall; that turn then ran out of time and got a random wall.

### Corrections to the day record
- Gap "whether stay is a free choice or only a move out and back": the rule text explicitly allows returning to where the marker was.

### Gaps
- The four-player rules are still not given.
- How far a marker may travel when breaking a wall, and whether the broken wall disappears, are still unclear.
- Whether the board edge can take a wall is not said; none of the logged moves of the two rounds puts one there.
- No tie-break for equal territory.

## Season 3 (Dead Again), Day 4: prison match
- **Name in the source:** Wall Go II (also "Wall Baduk 2")
- **Players:** 3 prisoners (Players A, B, C) in round 1; 2 of them in a duel in round 2.
- **Stakes:** the round 1 winner is safe; the round 2 loser is eliminated; every survivor gets 1 piece.

### Rules on the show
**Round 1 (3 players)**
- Each player has one square piece and one round piece.
- Square piece: moves 1 or 2 squares in one straight direction.
- Round piece: moves 1 square up, down, left or right, and may move 1 more square after turning left or right.
- Setup: in turn order each player places either their square or their round piece on any empty square; the second piece is placed in reverse order.
- Turn (90 seconds): move one of your pieces, then build a wall beside the moved piece. If time runs out, a wall is placed on a random side.
- A piece cannot move onto an occupied square or through a wall. A wall cannot be built where one already stands.
- Once per game, each player may break one wall that blocks the path of a move.
- **End:** at once when walls fully separate the areas holding the different players' pieces, even if wall breaks are unused.
- **Score:** total squares of the areas holding your pieces; the largest total is safe. Tie: compare the larger single area.

**Round 2 (duel)**
- The two players other than the round 1 survivor play. The one who secured more squares in round 1 moves first.
- Each has 2 square and 2 round pieces; the starting squares of the round pieces are fixed in advance.
- No wall breaking. Otherwise as in round 1. The loser is eliminated.

### How it went
- Round 1: Player A had hoped to ally with Player C, but a misunderstanding left A playing alone. A started in the weakest position and dominated from the middle of the game. C concentrated on attacking Player B; C also failed to secure an area of their own and was finally squeezed by both opponents.
- Round 2: C moved toward the centre on a turn when their round piece could have blocked B, which gave up about a third of the board. C then spent turns trying to recover and lost clearly to B's solid defence.

### Result
- Player A survived by winning round 1.
- Player B beat Player C in round 2 and survived.
- Player C was eliminated. A and B received 1 piece each.

### Differences from our version
- Pieces: ours has identical pieces moving 0-2 steps with one turn allowed; the show had a straight-moving square piece and a round piece with its own bent move.
- Staying put: ours allows 0 steps; the source describes moving a piece.
- Setup: ours uses fixed start squares; on the show players placed pieces themselves (except the round pieces in the duel).
- Wall breaking: once per player in the show's round 1; none in ours.
- Duel: the show added a second round with 4 pieces each; ours is a single game.
- Time limit: 90 seconds on the show; none in ours.
- Scoring: ours gives 100 + 20 per square and lets equal areas share a rank; the show used the larger single area as tie-break and only decided survival.

### Gaps
- Board size is not given.
- The source's move-by-move progress section is empty; no area counts or turn counts, and not said who moved first in the duel.
- The wall placement sentence is garbled (it mentions only upper and lower areas); "one side of the moved piece's square" is the likely meaning.
- How many squares the round piece must move (whether the second step is optional) is only loosely worded.
- The two assessments of Player C's round 1 play in the source partly disagree.
