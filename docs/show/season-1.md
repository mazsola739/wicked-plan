# Season 1, day by day

The frame of each day: games in order, hidden stages, who was sent where and who left. Recorded
from the NamuWiki episode guides in our own words; player letters are assigned separately in each
section. Index: [On the show](README.md).

## Season rules
Season 1, from its game-rules page.

### Frame
- 12 players, 6 nights and 7 days. The two players with the most pieces play the final; its winner is the champion.
- Any plan is allowed, lies and betrayal included; violence and theft are not. No unauthorised electronic devices.
- The winner may not share the prize money with other participants, and may not trade rewards outside the show for in-game actions.

### Pieces
- The game currency: a gold rectangular chip with a groove in the middle. Everyone starts with 1.
- Won or lost through game results; can buy in-game advantages and be used in negotiation and trade.
- A player left with none is eliminated at once.
- Transfers only during living time in the living building, not in the game building. In one case pieces briefly lent for study during a main match were not treated as a transfer.
- The pieces come in three subtly different groove and line patterns; all starting pieces share one pattern.

### Matches
- **Main match:** daytime, competitive, all surviving players; pieces are won or lost.
- **Prize match:** evening, cooperative, all participants must complete a shared task. Six in the season. The prize pot starts at 0 and is capped at 500 million won.

### Prison
- After the main match the two players with the fewest pieces are imprisoned. If three or more tie at the bottom, the player with the most pieces names who goes. If one is alone at the bottom and two or more tie next, the lowest goes and one of the tied is picked. (The prison paragraph puts it more briefly: the player with the most pieces nominates.)
- Inmates miss that evening's prize match and stay, without changing clothes, until the next main match.
- A two-person room, mixed genders possible, behind an iron door in the game building. Dinner is bread, milk and tangerines; breakfast is porridge. A portable toilet, no sink, a fixed ration of water for washing.
- Prison applies only up to the Day 5 imprisonment (ending the next morning); none on Days 6 and 7.
- **Cast puzzle:** one per day hidden in the prison, a new design each time, worth 1 piece when handed to the dealer solved on release; it may be passed to the other inmate (see `metal-cast-puzzle`).

### Places and daily life
- **Game building:** used for main and prize matches; a main hall open to all, four separate rooms and a dealer room entered when needed. Players cannot leave during a game.
- **Living building:** two floors with a common area and kitchen; six two-person bedrooms (two downstairs, for the oldest man and woman), roommates matched by age. Lights go out at 11 pm except mood lights and lamps. No talking where there are no cameras; the restroom is used one person at a time.
- Nine Men's Morris and the four-player three-in-a-row game are available in the living area (later used in the Day 7 final and the Day 6 prize match).

### Hidden stage
- Behind a wall outlet in the prison is a safe-style lock. The code comes from fitting the three piece patterns together and reading the letters formed where the grooved lines break: they spell "next year", so the code is 2024 (filming took place in 2023).
- Opening it reveals a secret passage to the secret room.
- One inmate per day may stake all their pieces on a game there: a win returns them with 10 more, a loss takes them all and eliminates the player.
- The game was Blind Gomoku (see `blind-gomoku`).

### Corrections to the day record
- Day 1: "match winners decide who goes to prison" is not the general rule. Prisoners are the players with the fewest pieces; the leaders choose only to break ties, which on Day 1 covered nearly everyone.

### Gaps
- The page has no rules for any main match, prize match or final game; only the hidden stage has its own rule block.
- No rule for a tie in pieces between the finalists, or for the benefit the leading finalist gets.
- The shape in which the pieces are fitted together is garbled in translation (the Day 4 record says an octahedron).
- The Day 3 side bet on prisoners is not mentioned.

## Day 1
- Opening: 12 players enter and are housed in two-person rooms in the living quarters. Any plan the games allow is permitted; violence and theft are not.
- Season-wide: everyone starts with 1 piece; pieces can be handed to other players; match winners decide who goes to prison; a successful prize match adds money to the prize pot; lights go out at 11 pm.
- Match 1, main match: `virus-game`. The two terrorists won (+4 pieces each); the fanatic also met his goal (+1).
- The terrorists chose two prisoners (Players A and B).
- Free time: players practised the board games in the living room, among them `nine-mens-morris` and `hidden-fourth-player` (practice only, no match), and played a casual flicking game.
- Match 2, prize match: `spinning-puzzle-wheel`, played by the 10 non-prisoners. Failed in round 8 of 10; prize money 0 won (50 million won was at stake). Two players earned pieces.
- Prison: Players A and B found a `metal-cast-puzzle` worth 1 piece if solved before the next main match; A solved it.
- Hidden stages: none found.
- Evening: some pieces were passed between players as thanks or alliance gifts; a three-player alliance formed and planned to grow to six.
- Went to prison: 2 players. Eliminated: nobody. Prize pot after the day: 0 won.

## Day 2
- Morning: alliances take shape; a four-player group with many pieces and a larger group around players with few. Two players noticed that piece tokens differ in look by how they were awarded (entry, main match, prize match).
- Match 1, main match: `rules-race`. The winner got +3 pieces; places 10-12 lost pieces.
- Season-wide: main matches can now take pieces away, and a player with none is eliminated.
- Eliminated: the 12th-placed player (Player A), the season's first; the others wrote farewell notes.
- Went to prison: two majority-alliance players, chosen by the main-match winner.
- Match 2, prize match: `memory-scene-quiz`, 9 players. Success: 50 million won.
- Prison: a prisoner found a door lock hidden behind a power outlet and told the other (a clue to something hidden; nothing solved that day). The `metal-cast-puzzle` was not solved.
- Hidden stages: none completed; the hidden lock was found.
- Evening: pieces were given between allies and promised partners.
- Prize pot after the day: 50 million won.

## Day 3
- Episodes 5-6.
- Morning: the previous night's prison puzzle had gone unsolved, so no reward.
- New for this day: a side bet on who would be imprisoned that day (1 piece per correct guess, no stake). The source says it was announced as something to continue but did not appear again.
- Main match: Secret Number (`secret-number`), 11 players.
- Eliminated in the main match: Players A and B (both lost their last pieces).
- Prison: Players C and D. Living quarters: Players E, F, G, H, I, J and K.
- Bet winners (all on C): E, H and I, 1 piece each.
- Hidden stage: nothing found yet; E and J kept trying to fit pieces of different shapes together.
- Prize match: Word Tower (`word-block-tower`), played by the 7 in the living quarters; success, +100 million won, total 150 million won; no piece changes.
- Pieces at day's end: E 4; F, G, H, I 3; C, J, K 2; D 1.
- Evening: the prison lamps stay lit after lights out.

## Day 4
- Episodes 7-8.
- Hidden stage: in the night Players A and B found that three differently shaped pieces fit together into an octahedron, a key to a deeper secret. A then wanted to get into the prison to follow it up.
- Prison secrets in circulation: Player C asked Player D not to tell others about a safe in the prison, expecting it to hold a protection against elimination; at night D told A of a hidden door lock there.
- Main match: Zoo (`animal-tile-auction`), 9 players.
- A borrowed pieces from B during the match to use on the secret in prison.
- After the main match: Player E gave C 1 piece in repayment for a ticket bought in the Day 3 main match.
- Prison: A and D (lowest, 1 piece each, assigned automatically). Living quarters: B, C, E and four others (Players F, G, H, I).
- Prize match: Scale Game (`weight-balance-puzzle`), 7 players; failed, no money added, total stays 150 million won, no piece changes.
- Evening: Player F gave Player G 1 piece in return for points G had given up so F could score.
- Prison game: cast puzzle (`metal-cast-puzzle`), solved by A.
- Eliminations: none.
- Pieces at day's end: C 4; B, E, F, G, H 3; A, I 2; D 1.

## Day 5
- Main match: Laying Grass (`polyomino-territory`), 9 players.
- Prize match: Montage (`duplicate-photo-buzzer`), six living-quarters players; success, prize money up to ₩250 million.
- Prison game: a cast puzzle (`metal-cast-puzzle`); the solver handed the reward right to the other prisoner.
- Hidden stage: the two prisoners used the password "2024", found hidden in the piece tokens (the assembled clue meant "next year"), to open a safe in the prison; a passage led to a secret room with a meaningful phrase. Several players had known of the safe's lock earlier. Challenge: Blind Gomoku (`blind-gomoku`), once a day.
- Prison: Players A and B. A had steered the end of the main match so as to go there and pursue the hidden stage.
- Eliminations: Player C (4th of the season) in the main match, left with 0 pieces; Player B (5th) by losing the secret-room game.
- Season-wide rule: the Day 5 main match fixed the prisoners, and from Day 6 the lowest ranked get no prison penalty.
- Pieces moved between players by gift or deal (one after the main match, two after the prize match); tile change coupons could be bought with pieces and passed on.
- In the evening the players guessed the coming games and practised board games.

## Day 6
- Hidden stage, in the morning: Blind Gomoku (`blind-gomoku`) in the secret room; Player A won, 3 pieces to 13.
- Main match: Equation High-Low (`equation-hi-lo`), 7 players, played until 3 remained; it lasted more than 7 hours.
- Prize match: Four Player Three in a Row (`hidden-fourth-player`), 3 players against a guest player; failed, prize money stays at ₩250 million.
- Before the main match the others loosely agreed to eliminate A first, expecting a prison reward.
- No prison penalty for the lowest ranked from this day on.
- Pieces after main match / after prize match: A 16 / 22; Player B 11 / 14; Player C 11 / eliminated.
- Eliminations: four players in the main match (6th to 9th of the season) and C in the prize match (10th).
- Finalists: A and B.

## Day 7
- The morning had only the two finalists; the eliminated players then came back to the living quarters, shared a meal and saw them off to the final.
- Final match between Player A (22 pieces) and Player B (14 pieces): up to three games, first to win two takes the season. The player with more pieces got a benefit in each game.
  1. Nine Men's Morris (`nine-mens-morris`), itself a best of three: A won 2–1. Benefit: choosing who moves first in its games 1 and 3.
  2. Hexagon (`hexagon`): A won 7–5. Benefit: a 1-point head start.
  3. Dice Poker, a betting game with a psychological side: not played, and its rules were not revealed.
- No prize match, prison or hidden stage on this day's page.
- Result: A is the champion with ₩250,000,000; B is runner-up.
