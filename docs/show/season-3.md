# Season 3: Dead Again, day by day

from the NamuWiki episode guides in our own words; player letters are assigned separately in each

## Season rules

Season 3 (Dead Again). Source: the Dead Again game-rules page, folded sections included. Only what adds to or corrects the day files.

### Rules added
- **Pieces:** one per player at the start. They can buy helpful game items or be used in negotiation. They can be handed over only inside the residence building.
- **Elimination:** the player or players eliminated in a main or prison match give up all their pieces.
- **Prison match:** every prisoner takes part.
- **Prize-money tokens:** earned from missions in the game building and the residence building, and activated by putting them into slots in the game building. 100 million won each, at most 5 tokens (500 million won).
- **Prize-token routes listed for the season:** 1 for a public-interest majority in choice 4 of Either/Or (Day 1); 1 for final assets of 10 million won or more in Mystery Exchange (Day 2); 2 from the secret safe in the living quarters; 1 as the optional reward of the exile trial (instead of 10 pieces); 1 more for a public-interest majority before HIT, STAY (Day 6). These add up to more than the cap.
- Betrayal and deceit are allowed; violence and theft are not. Outside electronic devices are banned during the game. The winner may not share the prize with other participants after filming, or offer off-show rewards for influencing the game.
- **Living quarters:** directly above the prison, with a glass floor and weak soundproofing; one shared sleeping space without separate rooms; varied meals and snacks; room for light exercise; a Wall Go II board is in the residence building.
- **Prison:** a narrow circular space; no personal belongings; porridge, kimchi and pickled radish for breakfast, bread and milk for dinner. Prison matches are played in a separate "death room" inside the prison building.
- **Secret safe (living quarters):** a bedside socket that does not work hides a hole for one piece, which lights a question mark on a wall; under the wallpaper there is a second hole, where 12 pieces assembled into an interlocking key (a "Dirty Dozen" cast puzzle) start a lift to the safe. Pieces used in the key are not used up. The safe holds two prize tokens behind a talisman; one person only may try, or the group may turn back.
- **Token of Salvation:** a holder who would be eliminated survives as a prisoner; a holder placed in the prison moves to the living quarters. Usable until just after the fifth main match. It can be passed on, but not to the player named in the curse. Using it cancels the Alliance Token used that day.

### Corrections to the day record
- Alliance Token and Token of Salvation (Days 1 and 4): on top of the day files' description, using the salvation token voids that day's Alliance Token move, which is why the Day 4 token receiver went back to prison.
- Curse (Day 1, bears on the Day 2 gap about a cursed player spending a piece): the page disagrees with itself. The curse notice says the cursed player loses all pieces at once and can no longer gain or use them; the page's own summary puts that loss only on failing the mission.

### Gaps
- What exactly sets off the curse is stated to be unknown.
- The game-rules page has no per-game rules and no exile-match rules. The exile page, folded text included, gives none for Piece Puzzle, Find the Differences, Secret Code, Fence Building or Symmetry Puzzle, and the rules box of Point Gomoku is empty.

## Day 1
- **Start:** 16 players, 1 piece each.
- **Season rules introduced:**
  - Pieces no longer decide who lives where, and running out of pieces does not eliminate.
  - Each day the main match eliminates at least one of its lowest-ranked players; then the top ranks go to the living quarters and the lower ranks to the prison. In the evening the prison match eliminates at least one more.
  - The main match winner gets the Alliance Token, which moves one prison-bound player to the living quarters.
  - Prize-money tokens, found through missions in the game and living buildings, each add to the final prize (stated as 100 million won per token).
  - The living quarters and the prison are separated by a glass floor, so each can see the other; meals differ (a full dinner against rye bread and milk).
- **Main match:** Either/Or (`either-or`), with mini-games Count the Cubes (`count-the-cubes`), Count the Rectangles (`count-the-rectangles`), Lay the Grass (`lay-the-grass`) and Memory Poker (`memory-poker`); one unnamed extra duel inside the third choice.
- **Main match outcome:** Players A (16th) and B (15th) eliminated. Winner C gave the Alliance Token to D.
- **Living quarters:** C, D, E, F, G, H, I.
- **Prison:** J, K, L, M, N, O, P.
- **Hidden stage (living quarters, at night):**
  - E found a device behind a power socket that accepted a piece and led to a hidden network of slots in a wall.
  - The slots needed many pieces assembled into one complex key (at least 12; the residents pooled 13). This opened the floor onto a secret lift down to a hidden safe.
  - Prize: 200 million won as a prize-money token behind a "curse barrier"; one representative could try, or turn back. The residents chose F.
- **Prize mission:** F had to take the token through an arm-sized hole between stone stacks topped with bells, knocked the stacks over and got the token, which triggered the curse.
  - Curse: lose all pieces and never gain or use pieces again, unless lifted.
  - To lift it: secretly name a living-quarters resident (not oneself) who must be eliminated in the next main match. F named E.
  - Reward on success: a salvation token that can save its holder from elimination or move a prisoner to the living quarters; usable until right after the fifth main match, transferable, but not to the named player.
  - F did not tell the others the true terms.
- **Prison match (8 p.m.):** Heavy 13 (`heavy-13`). L won 4 pieces; O (6th) and J (7th) were eliminated.
- **Eliminated on the day:** A and B (main match), O and J (prison match). 12 remain.
- **Prize money shown at the end of the day:** 0 won.
- **Exile:** eliminated players' further route is on a separate Exile page.
- **Gaps:** the exact wording of the curse and salvation token is only partly clear in translation; when the 200 million won is counted is not stated (the Day 2 total includes it).

## Exile (the hidden stage, Days 1 to 6)
Season-wide exile rules:
- The exile place appears late in episode 5 and is shown in detail from episode 6. An eliminated player picks the blue door (go home) or the red door (exile, with a chance to return).
- At most 4 people stay. Exile matches are held just before the main and prison match eliminations are settled, to keep the number down; losers are finally eliminated.
- Two ways back: pass God's test (`magic-square`; before a main match, 1 hour; reward 10 pieces or a prize-money token), or survive to win the last exile match on Day 6.
- The rules must first be deciphered from the exile language (`unknown-language`).

By day (letters count arrivals in order):
- Day 1: A and B arrive after the main match; C and D after the prison match.
- Day 2: exile match 01, `piece-puzzle` (B out). E arrives after the main match. Exile match 02, `find-the-differences` (A and D out). F and G arrive after the prison match.
- Day 3: exile match 03, `secret-code` (E out). H arrives after the main match. Exile match 04, `fence-building` (C out). Nobody arrives after the prison match (the player eliminated there could not join after a panic attack).
- Day 4: F takes God's test and fails. I arrives after the main match. Exile match 05, `symmetry-puzzle` (H out). J arrives after the prison match.
- Day 5: G has passed God's test and returns with 10 pieces. K arrives from the main match; exile match 06, `flags-of-the-world` (I out). L arrives from the prison match; exile match 07, `count-the-rectangles` (J out).
- Day 6: exile match 08, `point-gomoku`, K against L: K returns with 5 pieces, L is out. Exile closes; later eliminations are final.
