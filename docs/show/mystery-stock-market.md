## Season 3 (Dead Again), Day 2: main match
- **Name in the source:** Mystery Exchange (Mystery Stock Market)
- **Players:** 12, each for themselves; two open alliances (4 and 8)
- **Stakes:** ranks 1-4 to the living quarters, 5-11 to the prison, 12th eliminated; 1st gets the Alliance Token; pieces for large fortunes and a prize-money token for 10 million won

### Rules on the show
- Seven companies, each starting at 10,000 won; a price never falls below 100 won.
- Each player has a terminal and 100,000 won of virtual cash.
- 24 rounds of 5 minutes. In a round players buy and sell and can see their own holdings and the current prices.
- At the start of each round everyone sees the new prices, each stock's trading (shares bought and sold) and total shares held, and every player's assets and rank.
- Each stock has its own price rule. Only part of each rule's wording is visible at the start (blanks keep the spacing and character count). More is revealed at the start of rounds 5, 10 and 15, and everything at round 20. The wording to be revealed next is marked in another colour.
- **Secret:** once per game a player may hide one stock. Its price, trading and holdings are not shown for the two rounds after the round of use. If several players use it on the same stock at once, it works once and all those Secrets are spent. While a Secret is active, ranks are shown but assets are not.
- **Pieces** (usable until the end of round 20): pay 1 piece to see early the next information to be revealed for one chosen item; or exchange 1 piece for 10,000 won of virtual cash.
- After round 24 total assets decide the ranking. A tie for last: the player whose total assets during the game were lowest is eliminated.
- Rewards by final assets: 1.5 million won or more, 1 piece; over 3 million, 2 pieces; over 6 million, 3 pieces; 10 million or more, a prize-money token.

**The seven rules as finally revealed** (wording in our own words):
1. NotSoGood Capital: next round +20% if four or more stocks rise; next round -10% if four or more fall; the price history also has unchanged rounds.
2. Mucho Construction: +10% if Banger Steel's price rose, otherwise -10%.
3. Banger Steel: -200 won for each share by which its total holdings grow, +200 won for each share by which they shrink.
4. Alt Ent: follows a fixed price curve shaped like the exchange's logo.
5. Open Security: +13% if a Secret is on Analog Electronics, otherwise -1,000 won.
6. CoyoTech: +1,400 won if more CoyoTech shares than Analog Electronics shares were bought, otherwise -700 won.
7. Analog Electronics: +1,500 won if its total holdings are 10 to 30 shares, otherwise it falls (see Gaps).

### How it went
- Early guesses: rules react to trading and to other stocks, with related industries linked.
- Players A, B, C and D formed a four-person alliance. Players E and F, with the four's agreement, acted as go-betweens and joined a second alliance that Player G, coming from the prison side, had proposed. The eight were E, F, G, H, I, J, K and L.
- Pieces: A converted 4 into cash in round 5 and H 2 in round 19. Early information was bought by K (round 7), B, J and L (round 10), I (round 15) and F (round 16).
- Secrets: Analog Electronics stayed hidden on every round report from round 5 to 17 and again in rounds 22 and 23; Banger Steel and NotSoGood Capital were hidden briefly in rounds 14-16. Assets were therefore hidden for most of the game.
- A led from the round 5 report to the end.
- Open Security climbed steeply while Analog Electronics was hidden (about 7,000 won at round 4 to a peak near 38,700 won at round 23). Analog Electronics fell to the 100 won floor by round 21, and several hundred thousand of its shares were traded in the last stretch. Alt Ent was bought heavily near its low around rounds 19-20 and sold after its rise.
- I, who had to get E eliminated (hidden-stage curse), falsely told E that E could only survive by finishing last. E lowered E's own total over several rounds and dropped to 12th by the round 21 report.
- The four-person alliance tried to lift F into the top four, but the last purchase failed.

### Differences from our version
- Show: seven stocks with linked, fixed rules whose wording is uncovered step by step. Ours: rules drawn from a list of ten, one private clue each, one more rule public after rounds 8 and 16.
- Show makes assets, ranks and buy/sell volumes public; ours hides others' cash and holdings and shows net trades and number of holders.
- Show has the Secret ability, pieces (information or cash), a 5-minute round clock, rewards and an elimination; ours has none and ends a round when all tap Done.
- Show: 12 players; ours: 3-8 seats.

### Gaps
- The translation of rule 1 says "four or fewer" fall for -10%; the price history only fits "four or more".
- The translation of rule 7 gives -1,500 won as the other case, but visible price steps outside the range are -1,000 won.
- The partly hidden early wording does not survive translation; which item a paid piece could reveal is not detailed.
- At what price trades execute, and the final prices after round 24, are not listed.
- The page has no round-by-round account of negotiations; much above comes from its price and ranking tables.

## Season 3 (Dead Again), game-rules page

The game-rules page adds nothing about this game beyond the 10 million won prize mission. The points below come from the folded price, trading and ranking tables of the Day 2 page (reports 2 to 24 and a final column).

### Rules added
- Each round report lists, per company, the price with a rise or fall mark, the shares held by all players together, and the shares bought and sold. A company under Secret shows question marks for all of these.
- The price in a report already follows the holdings and trading shown in that same report.
- The ranking table has a column after round 24, labelled round 25 (final), which gives the result. Compared with the bracketed round 24 assets, the final assets are lower for the players who finished 1st (3,660,902 to 3,563,702), 5th (1,979,076 to 1,957,476), 6th (1,586,899 to 1,547,899) and 9th (1,127,197 to 1,027,297); the other eight are unchanged.

### Corrections to the day record
- Rule 7 (Analog Electronics): the step outside the 10 to 30 share range is -1,000 won, as the visible reports show (4 and 6 shares held: -1,000 each; 15 held: +1,500; none held in reports 19 and 20: -1,000 each; 197 held in report 21: down to the 100 won floor). The -1,500 in the translated rule text does not fit.
- Rule 1 (NotSoGood Capital): "four or more fall" is borne out; after report 7, where at most three companies can have fallen, its price stayed the same. This confirms the day record's reading.
- Rules 2, 3, 5 and 6 as recorded agree with the visible price steps checked.

### Gaps
- Still not stated: the price at which trades are filled, the prices behind the final column, and why only four players' assets changed in it (a last price move is the likely reason).
- The early, partly hidden rule wording is still not available.
