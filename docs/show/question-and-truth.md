## Season 2 (Death Room), Day 7: final, game 3
- **Name in the source:** Question and Truth
- **Players:** the 2 finalists. Player A (14 pieces), Player B (11 pieces).
- **Stakes:** the deciding game; the winner takes the season and 380 million won. The piece benefit for this game was not broadcast.

### Rules on the show
- Each player has a deck of 52 playing cards (no jokers) and 10 tokens.
- **Layout:** each chooses 8 cards and lays them in a row. Cards of the same suit must rise from left to right (they need not be neighbours). Ace = 1, J = 11, Q = 12, K = 13. The row is fixed for the whole game.
- **Each turn:** both secretly submit any number of their tokens within 30 seconds. The higher bid acts: one question or one truth. Submitted tokens are spent by both sides, and each player then receives 2 new ones.
- Equal bids: nobody acts; the tokens are collected, both get 2 and bid again.
- **Warning light:** lit when a player has fewer than 5 tokens; it goes out once they have 6 or more.
- **Question** (the opponent must answer truthfully; any may be repeated):

| No. | Group | Asks |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Sum | sum of three cards of the asker's choice (by position) |
| 2 | Sum | sum of the cards of a chosen suit |
| 3 | Sum | sum of the letter cards (A, J, Q, K) |
| 4 | Sum | sum of the number cards |
| 5 | Count | how many letter cards |
| 6 | Count | how many number cards |
| 7 | Count | how many of one number or letter |
| 8 | Position | positions of one suit |
| 9 | Position | positions of cards with the same number or letter |
| 10 | Position | positions of cards with consecutive numbers or letters |
| 11 | Position | position of the largest or smallest number |

- **Truth:** declare the opponent's 8 values from left to right; suits are not needed. All right wins the game; any mistake and play goes on to the next turn.
- No time limit for asking or declaring (mentioned only behind the scenes).

### How it went
- Layouts: A had D2, H5, C6, H8, HK, S4, D10, C8. B had CA, D8, C3, D10, C5, CQ, H7, HQ.
- Turns 1 to 15 were all questions after unequal bids: both spent them first on suits, letter cards and equal or consecutive values, then on position sums. A asked 10 questions in the game, B 11.
- Sample answers: "consecutive cards" about B gave positions 2 and 7 (8 and 7); "same value" gave positions 6 and 8 (the two queens).
- Turn 16 was the first tie; ties came back in turns 22, 23, 26 and 27.
- Turn 25: A bid 6 to 4 and declared first, with one value wrong (a writing slip).
- Turn 28: B declared and was wrong.
- From turn 29 both ran dry and bid the same amounts again and again (4 each, then 2 each), a deadlock lasting past turn 33; the rest was cut.
- A, sure of the answer, broke it by bidding 0 to build up a certain majority next turn, gambling that B's declaration would miss. B bid 2, declared A's row correctly and won.

### Result
- Player B won the game and the final 2 to 1, taking 380 million won. Player A was runner-up.

### Differences from our version
- Show: players choose and arrange 8 of 52 cards with the suit-order rule. Ours: a random row of 1 to 8.
- Show: sealed token bids each turn decide who acts; 2 tokens refill. Ours: alternating turns, fixed question costs, no refill.
- Show: sums, counts and positions. Ours: card at position, position of a card, sum of two, and three yes/no questions.
- Show: a wrong declaration only loses that turn; the first right one wins. Ours: one final guess each, scored per position plus bonuses.
- Show: low token counts are signalled by a light. Ours: both counts open.

### Gaps
- Whether token counts were hidden from the opponent apart from the warning light is implied, not stated.
- Penalties for a false answer are not described.
- Question 2's wording is garbled; the log shows it was used as a suit sum.
- Questions 4, 6 and 11 were not used, so their exact reach is unclear.
- No limit on repeated ties; the producer mentioned a reserve game.
- The warning light's off threshold is translated as "more than six" and also fits "6 or more".
