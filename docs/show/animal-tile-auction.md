are Player A, B …; letters are assigned separately in each section. **Gaps** lists what the source

## Season 1, Day 4: main match
- **Name in the source:** Zoo
- **Players:** 9, each for themselves; alliances formed
- **Stakes:** pieces by final points; the two lowest (1 piece each) ended up in prison

### Rules on the show
- Turn order is drawn by lot. There is a 30-minute preparation period.
- Animal cards: a deck of 30, six each of snake, parrot, elephant, lion and monkey. Each player draws 3 face down, chooses their order and hands in that three-animal combination.
- Condition cards: 10, each comparing two animals (more of the first than of the second on the board): lion/snake, snake/elephant, lion/monkey, snake/monkey, parrot/lion, elephant/parrot, elephant/snake, monkey/snake, parrot/elephant, monkey/lion. Each player draws one.
- Each player must then reveal one animal of their combination. The other animals, the order and the condition stay secret.
- Board: 5 by 5. Placed tiles drop from the top and fill each column from the bottom.
- Five rounds, each: tile draw, auction, board update. Two tile groups, A and B, of 20 animal tiles each; every round 4 tiles are drawn and shown for each group.
- Auction: players enter the dealer room one by one, in an order that alternates between reverse and forward (round 1 reverse). Each picks group A or B, bids credits and states how they would place that group's 4 tiles.
- Everyone starts with 30 credits. Others' balances are hidden; credits cannot be traded, moved or destroyed. A bid is at least 1 credit and may be everything left. Bid credits are spent even if the bid loses.
- The group that received fewer credits in total wins. Its highest bidder wins and their placement is carried out. The other group's tiles leave the game.
- If the two totals are equal, or the top bid in the winning group is tied, the auction is void: credits are returned and it is held again.
- The winner is not announced to everyone. After each round the total credits used and the resulting board are shown.
- Scoring: 1 point for every straight line (row, column or diagonal, read in either direction) where your combination appears in order. Meeting your condition doubles your points.
- Pieces: 2 points or more +1; 1 point no change; 0 points -1; a sole first place earns 3 more.

### How it went
- Order, combination, condition, revealed animal, pieces at the start:
  - Player A: elephant-snake-elephant; parrot over elephant; snake; 1
  - Player B: monkey-lion-monkey; snake over elephant; lion; 4
  - Player C: lion-snake-snake; lion over monkey; lion; 3
  - Player D: monkey-parrot-elephant; elephant over snake; monkey; 3
  - Player E: parrot-lion-elephant; snake over monkey; parrot; 2
  - Player F: monkey-snake-elephant; monkey over snake; snake; 3
  - Player G: elephant-parrot-monkey; elephant over parrot; parrot; 2
  - Player H: parrot-lion-parrot; parrot over lion; lion; 2
  - Player I: lion-parrot-monkey; monkey over lion; lion; 3
- One lion and two snake cards, and one condition card (lion/snake), were not dealt. D and G held the same combination, reversed.
- E secretly allied with I aiming for a sole win by E; E, H and I also compared their shared parrot-lion pairs.
- Board columns are numbered 1 to 5 from the left; tiles are listed bottom to top.
- **Round 1** (41 credits used): group A snake, elephant, snake, lion; group B elephant, parrot, elephant, snake. B won group A with 8 (C bid 7; most others bid 3 or 4, many on B). Column 2 got snake, snake, lion, elephant, which gave C a line. C had offered help to others in exchange. D and G failed to agree because G kept the combination vague.
- **Round 2** (59 used): group A parrot, lion, monkey, elephant; group B lion, monkey, monkey, snake. E won A with 13. G, who wanted B, put 10 on A to inflate it, but E outbid that. Column 3 got elephant, lion, parrot, monkey, scoring for E and I.
- **Round 3** (60 used): group A elephant, snake, monkey, parrot; group B lion, lion, monkey, lion. F won A with 13 (G bid 12 on A). Column 1 got monkey, snake, elephant, parrot. It came out that several players had misunderstood whether auctioned tiles leave the game and how many tiles there were. D's alliance had failed to pass H's combination on. C wanted to outbid F to help H but feared A's total would get too high. D chose to save A, who had one piece, and asked H to give up the chance in A's favour; H agreed.
- **Round 4** (43 used): group A parrot, monkey, monkey, elephant; group B snake, elephant, parrot, elephant. A won B with 13: snake in column 4; elephant, parrot, elephant in column 5. C, angry that D had put saving others ahead of the original four-player pact (C, D, E, H), said so openly; E agreed.
- **Round 5** (60 used): group A lion, snake, parrot, lion; group B parrot, parrot, monkey, snake. C expected to win, but I, who had saved credits (only 1 in each of rounds 3 and 4), bid 11 on A and took it, placing in E's favour: snake and lion on column 4, parrot on column 5, lion on column 2. E had misjudged C's condition, so E and C finished level and the sole-first bonus was lost.
- G, with no way left to score, borrowed pieces from B during the match to work on the hidden secret in prison.

### Result
- Points: C 6, E 6, A 3, F 2, I 1, B 0, D 0, G 0, H 0.

### Differences from our version
- Show: 9 players; ours 3 to 6 seats.
- Show: five animals and an ordered three-animal combination, one animal of it public, plus a comparison condition that doubles points; ours four animals, a fully secret pattern, no condition.
- Show: straight lines only, either direction; ours allows L shapes and rotations without mirroring.
- Show: empty board, tiles fall to the bottom of a column; ours starts with 5 random tiles and the winner picks any empty cell.
- Show: 5 auctions, each between two groups of 4 tiles; ours 12 auctions of one tile.
- Show: the less-funded group wins, its top bidder places, and every bid is spent; ours the single highest bidder wins and is the only one to pay.
- Show: a tie voids the auction and repeats it; ours discards the tile.
- Show: 30 hidden credits; ours 15 chips, shown to all.
- Show: 1 point per line, doubled by the condition, with pieces as reward; ours 25 per copy, 5 per animal of a partial match, 1 per chip.

### Gaps
- Many bids and most losing placements were not aired.
- The guide's player table gives A the condition "lion over elephant", which is not in its own list of cards; "parrot over elephant" is inferred. A's condition was not met either way.
- The guide's board graphic shows a lion in the third row of column 1; the placement and group tiles say elephant, and the scores fit elephant.
- The guide's result table swaps parrot and monkey for E and I; the combinations above follow its other tables and the placements.
- "More of the first animal than the second" is how the comparison reads; the card wording is not given.
- Whether every player had to bid in every round is unclear.
- The exact size of the sole-first bonus comes from a garbled line; nobody earned it.
