## Season 3 (Dead Again), Day 4: main match
- **Name in the source:** Auction Bingo
- **Players:** 8: Players A-G and a guest player. Informal sides: A, B, C, D from the living side; E, F, G and the guest with the prison side.
- **Stakes:** the guest is left out of the ranking for rewards. Places 1-2 to the living quarters, 3-6 to the prison, 7th eliminated; first place gets the Alliance Token; a completely filled board earns 4 pieces. The guest could win 10 million won for finishing first and another 10 million won for filling the board.

### Rules on the show
- Each player has a 3x3 board with a fixed shape on every square, the same for everyone: top row triangle, circle, triangle; middle row circle, star, circle; bottom row star, circle, triangle (3 triangles, 4 circles, 2 stars).
- Each player has 7 name tickets and 30 coin units in indivisible coins: three 1s, three 2s, two 3s, one 5 and one 10, kept in the dealer room. The guest started with 1 piece.
- A piece can be used as a coin worth 1.
- **7 rounds**, each in three steps.
  1. **Shape choice** (15 minutes): put one name ticket into the box of one shape, in the open hall. All choices are revealed once everyone has submitted.
  2. **Bid** (20 minutes): privately in the dealer room, bid coins worth at least 1 on the chosen shape. A player who cannot bid becomes the lowest bidder automatically.
  3. **Announcement:** for each shape the highest bidder wins as many of that shape as there were players who chose it. Tied top bidders split them equally; any remainder is discarded.
- Coins that were bid are gone, win or lose.
- Winners are not named, not even to themselves. Only the winning bid of each shape is announced, and the largest of the three is kept secret.
- After round 7 the won shapes are revealed and placed automatically on each board to make as many bingo lines as possible; surplus shapes are discarded.
- **Ranking:** most bingo lines, then most filled squares, then most coins left.

### How it went
- The prison side planned to bring down Player D; Players A and B privately considered joining. The guest said he would back the prison side and not try to win, yet also helped D at times, wanting to protect whoever was outnumbered. D and the guest agreed to take turns submitting their ticket first (guest in odd rounds, D in even ones).

- Round 1: several pairs agreed to split a shape cheaply. The guest, while signalling that his advice was false, urged G to bid 18 as a loyalty test of E; G bid only 3 and lost the stars to D.
- Round 2: G broke a sharing promise to C and took 3 circles, then feigned surprise.
- Round 3: A broke a one-coin agreement with G. C and F tied at 19, and C at first believed all four circles were theirs.
- Round 4: D took 3 triangles for 2 after the guest bid only 1, which broke the plan against D and angered the prison side. B broke a two-coin sharing deal with E, and G paid A back for Round 3 by bidding 12 on the stars.
- Round 5: the guest talked of bidding 8 on triangles, scaring others into bidding 1, then bid 2 and won 4. E hinted at a low bid, bid 6 and took the stars from A.
- Round 6: D gave up a chance to complete the board and left the circles to A, expecting the guest to go all in only in round 7. The guest had implied he bid 3 but had bid all 23 and took the circles.
- Round 7: with the dealer's earlier permission to insert other items without touching the boxes, the guest dropped his ticket (wrapped in paper) and two blank decoys into all three boxes, so no one knew his choice; it turned out to be the star box. Everyone spent all remaining coins. D won the circles and completed the board.

### Differences from our version
- Boards: ours are secret, random, with 3 of each shape; the show gave everyone the same known layout with 3 triangles, 4 circles and 2 stars.
- Choice: ours picks a particular empty cell; the show chose only a shape, publicly, and placed everything at the end.
- Ours shows only how many picked each shape; on the show the choices were revealed.
- Prize: ours marks one cell; the show winner got as many shapes as there were choosers.
- Ties: ours gives nobody the shape; the show split the shapes.
- Coins: ours 20, freely divisible, bid 0 or more; the show 30 in fixed coins, at least 1 per round, plus pieces as extra coins.
- Payment: ours charges only the winner; on the show every bid was spent.
- Information: ours announces every winning bid and tells you if you won; the show hid the largest winning bid and never told the winner.
- Score: ours adds points for lines, cells and coins; the show ranked by lines, then cells, then coins.
- Seats: ours 3-8 (page: 4); the show had 8 including a guest.

### Gaps
- The source does not say whether diagonals count as lines (the tied ranks fit rows, columns and diagonals) and does not list line counts.
- Not said whether the choices were revealed with names or only as visible tickets in the boxes (tickets carried names).
- Not said how many pieces a player could add as coins in one round.
- Whether a surplus coin tie-break was ever needed is not mentioned.
