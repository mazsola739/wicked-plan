# Nine Men's Morris on the show

How this game was played on The Devil's Plan. Our version: [rules page](../games/nine-mens-morris.md).
Recorded from the NamuWiki episode guides (machine-translated), in our own words. Contestants
are Player A, B …; letters are assigned separately in each section. **Gaps** lists what the source
leaves open. Index: [On the show](README.md).

## Season 1, Day 7: final, game 1
- **Name in the source:** Nine Men's Morris.
- **Players:** the two finalists, Player A (22 pieces) and Player B (14 pieces).
- **Stakes:** the first of up to three final games; two final games won take the season and ₩250 million.

### Rules on the show
- Board of 24 points; 9 black pieces for one player, 9 white for the other.
- Players take turns placing one piece on an empty point until all are placed; 90 seconds for a placement. No piece moves before then.
- Then each turn moves a piece along a line to a neighbouring empty point.
- Three of your pieces on three points joined in a straight line let you remove one opposing piece; a piece standing in such a three cannot be removed.
- A player with only 3 pieces may move to any empty point.
- A player loses when none of their pieces can move or when down to 2 pieces.
- A stalemate rule: in a late endgame, if it drags on for more than 10 turns, the player with fewer pieces loses.
- Played as a best of three: the first to win two games takes this final game.
- **Piece benefit:** the player with more pieces decides who moves first in games 1 and 3.

### How it went
- **Game 1** (A moved first): both chased threes more than a long plan. B made the first one on turn 6, and captures went back and forth: 7–7 after turn 12, 5–5 after turn 20, A 3 to B 4 after turn 24, 3–3 after turn 33. The rest is not recorded; B won.
- **Game 2** (B moved first): B made the first three on turn 7. A answered on turn 10, then captured on turns 13, 15, 17 and 19 while keeping B's pieces hemmed in at the centre. With A 8 to B 4, B could not stop the repeated threes and lost.
- **Game 3** (A moved first): a careful opening. A captured on turns 13, 15, 18, 19, 21, 23 and 25 without losing a piece; after B's own slip, A won with 9 pieces against 2.

### Result
- A won the final game 2–1.

### Differences from our version
- Show: best of three; ours: a single game.
- Show: the piece leader picks who starts games 1 and 3; ours: you start.
- Show: 90 seconds per turn; ours: no timer.
- Show: an endgame stalemate rule of about 10 turns; ours: draw at 200 turns, or 60 turns without a removal.
- Our page allows removing from a three when all enemy pieces are in threes; the source mentions no exception.
- Ours scores points; the show only win or loss.

### Gaps
- The stalemate rule is badly translated: which piece counts trigger it is unclear.
- The text does not say whether the 90 seconds also applies to moves.
- The ends of games 1 and 3 are partly edited out; game 2's record stops before a formal finish.
- A few moves in game 3 are missing or unreadable.
- Colour labels for first and second player are garbled in the tables.
