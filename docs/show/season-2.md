# Season 2: Death Room, day by day

The frame of each day: games in order, hidden stages, who was sent where and who left. Recorded
from the NamuWiki episode guides in our own words; player letters are assigned separately in each
section. Index: [On the show](README.md).

## Season rules
Season 2 (Death Room), from its game-rules page, folded notes included.

### Frame
- 14 players, 6 nights and 7 days.
- Right after the Day 6 main match the player with the most pieces goes straight to the final. The other survivors play a prison match, and its winner is the second finalist. The winner of the final is the champion.
- Any plan is allowed, lies and betrayal included; violence and theft are not. No unauthorised electronic devices.
- The winner may not pass prize money to other participants or trade rewards outside the show for in-game actions. (The page marks the device and prize rules as confirmed in a Season 1 interview.)
- Props may not be damaged or altered, and a lost prop is not replaced.

### Pieces
- The game currency; every player gets pieces at the start and wins or loses them through game results. They buy in-game advantages and serve in negotiation and trade.
- A player left with none is eliminated at once. The pieces of an eliminated player vanish.
- Transfers only during living time, in the living building or the prison wing; never in the game building or the death room.
- All pieces share one design this season.

### Matches
- **Main match:** daytime, competitive, all surviving players.
- **Prize mission:** a different task inside each main match; success adds prize money. The pot starts at 0 and is capped at 500 million won.
- **Prison match:** evening, all players in the prison wing; it decides who is eliminated. The rules are opened at 8 pm, play starts after 30 minutes of strategy time, and the living quarters watch live.

### Living quarters and prison
- After the main match the surviving players are split by pieces held: the upper half to the living building, the rest to the prison wing. With an odd number the extra player goes to prison.
- If a tie at the dividing line prevents an even split, the player with the most pieces decides who goes to prison; if several share the most pieces, they decide by agreement.
- Nobody may leave their quarters until the next day's main match.
- Prisoners may keep no personal belongings (writing tools and food included).

### Places
- **Game building:** entered only for main matches, with no leaving during a game; side rooms are a kitchen, a meeting room, a winery and a warehouse. The restroom is used one person at a time.
- **Living building:** two bedrooms for two and one for three, storage and washing facilities, broadcast cameras. Luggage is stowed in built-in closets during games and rooms are assigned afterwards.
- **Prison wing:** at most seven people, only basic lodging under the guards, prison clothing, showers in the men's and women's restrooms. No reward puzzles this season, unlike Season 1.
- **Death room:** a separate room in the prison wing for the prison matches, a dome with mirrors all round; several players compete there, not just two.
- Both wings have two board games: Wall Go for two or four players (see `wall-territory`) and the solo knight's tour (see `knights-tour`).

### Hidden stages
- One in each wing, each for a single challenger.
- **Living building:** reached through puzzles starting at the bedroom clocks; the game was the knight's tour. One chance only; failure does not eliminate; success gives a strong advantage toward the final. Details in `knights-tour`.
- **Prison wing:** reached through a manhole under a floor panel; a life-or-death challenge. A failed challenger is eliminated and another may try after 24 hours; success closes the stage. Details in `rising-water-well`.

### Corrections to the day record
- Day 1, living-quarters clues: the idea that the four slots behind the gym picture needed four different piece designs was the players' reasoning at the time. The solution needs a single piece, put halfway into the second slot from the left (as the Day 2 record has it).

### Gaps
- The page has no rule block for any main match, prison match or final game. Apart from the two hidden stages, the per-game rules are only on the day pages.
- The number of starting pieces is not stated on this page (the Day 1 record says 1 each).
- No rule for a tie in pieces for the direct place in the final after Day 6.
- The finals' piece benefit is not described on this page.

## Day 1

- Season start: 14 players, 1 piece each (new piece design).
- Season-wide rules introduced: after each main match the top half of survivors live in the living quarters and the rest go to prison; the prize match is dropped; each main match carries a prize mission; every evening the prisoners play a prison match and one is eliminated.
- Free time before the first game; some early alliances formed.
- Main match: Corrupt Police (`thieves-and-cops`). The two thieves reached 12 pieces in round 11 of 15; the two thieves and two corrupt officers ended with 4 pieces each, the other ten with 1.
- Prize mission: finish by round 10 for 100 million won, minus 20 million per later round. Earned 80 million won; cumulative fund 80 million won.
- Living quarters (7): the four winners and three players they picked from those on 1 piece (after bargaining and a game of chance, with one place passed on). Prison (7): the rest.
- Living-quarters hidden clues: a wrong clock held a five-letter word lock; a letter die seen through a peephole gave the letters, and inside were a wrench and a note about birds on a tree. The wrench opened a tree picture in the gym with slots for four pieces of different designs; with only one design available it stayed unsolved.
- Prison match: Remove One (`minus-one`), 7 players; one eliminated (first of the season), one winner got 1 piece.
- Hidden stage found in prison: a floor panel hid a manhole worked with the cell handle as a hex wrench and an eight-letter word; water poured into a "water supply" hole floated up a ball with a riddle, answered "tomorrow". The message inside: one entrant only; success closes the stage; failure eliminates the challenger and another may try after 24 hours. Nobody entered that day.
- Board games lying in both wings: the knight's tour (`knights-tour`) and Wall Go (`wall-territory`). Wall Go was only tried casually; players practised the knight's tour late into the night in both wings.
- Elimination: 1; 13 survivors.
- Pieces at the end of the day: four players on 4, one on 2, eight on 1.

## Day 2

- Covers episodes 3-5. Start: 13 players.
- Morning, living quarters: one 1-piece player proposed an alliance to the other two on 1 piece.
- Prison hidden stage (`rising-water-well`), before the main match: one volunteer failed and was eliminated; 12 survivors.
- Main match: Unknown (`hidden-rules-race`), 12 players. Winner +3 pieces, 2nd to 5th +1 each; nobody eliminated.
- Prize mission: exactly one player eliminated in the main match for 100 million won. Failed; cumulative fund stays 80 million won.
- Living quarters (6): the five players on 4 or more pieces, plus one of the four on 2 pieces, chosen by the piece leader. One living-quarters player moved to prison. Prison (6): the rest.
- Pieces can be handed over only between players in the same wing. The piece leader then gave 1 piece to the player chosen for the sixth place.
- Living-quarters hidden stage found: a piece slotted into the second hole of the gym device (the patterns spelled "insert") opened a white room with one piece and a screen. One entrant, one chance, no elimination.
- Prison match: Time Auction (`time-auction`), 6 players; the newcomer from the living quarters was eliminated, the token leader got 1 piece.
- After the match: the living-quarters challenger passed the knight's tour (`knights-tour`) hidden stage; the reward (a claim on 10 extra pieces) stayed secret for now.
- Prison: four players wanted the next try at the prison hidden stage; rock-paper-scissors picked one for Day 3. Distrust grew inside the prison group over deals in the main match and a three-player pact in the prison match.
- Eliminations: 2 (hidden stage, prison match); 11 survivors.
- Pieces at the end of the day: five players on 4, two on 3, two on 2, two on 1.

## Day 3
- Source: Death Room day 3 guide (episodes 5 to 7).
- Hidden stage, prison quarters (morning): the water-filled well, `rising-water-well`. Player A cleared it, won 10 pieces (11 in hand) and gave 1 to Player B.
- Hidden stage, living quarters: Player C had earlier told Player A it was Wall Go, then let slip that it was the Knight's Tour (`knights-tour`). C shared the reward secretly with two allies after dinner; the guide says its content comes out later. Player D is credited with finding the entrance.
- Main match: Halloween Monster (`monster-alliance`), 11 players. Player E was killed under the hidden rule and eliminated.
- Prize mission: 100 million won if one player is eliminated in the main match; completed. Cumulative prize 180 million won.
- Living quarters (5): Players A, C, D, F, G. The last place went by the top piece holder's choice among three tied players. The living group then redistributed the alliance's pieces among themselves.
- Prison (5): Players B, H, I, J, K.
- Prison match: Sniper Hold'em (`sniper-holdem`), 68 rounds. Player J was eliminated; no pieces awarded.
- Eliminations: E (main match), J (prison match). 9 players remain.
- Pieces at the end of the day: A 12; C, D, F, G 6 each; H 4; I 4; B 2; K 1; E 0; J 0.
- Season rules seen in action: a player who loses every piece is out, also during a main match (first time this season).

## Day 4
- Source: Death Room day 4 guide (episodes 7 to 9).
- Hidden stages: none reported for this day.
- Main match: Treasure Island (`treasure-island`), 9 players. Player A found the treasure and took 4 pieces from three prison players. Player B was eliminated after the low-score penalty took B's only piece.
- Prize mission: 100 million won if only one player is eliminated in the main match; completed. Cumulative prize 280 million won.
- Piece penalties for low scores were announced openly in a main match for the first time this season.
- Living quarters (4): Players A, C, D, E. C, with the most pieces, was safe along with A; among three tied players, C chose who went to prison.
- Prison (4): Players F, G, H, I.
- Prison match: Triple Dice (`dice-prediction`). Player F won 1 piece; Player I was eliminated.
- Eliminations: B (main match), I (prison match). 7 players remain.
- Pieces at the end of the day: C 13; A 11; D 8; E 8; F 4; G 2; H 2; B 0; I 0.

## Day 5
- Episodes 9 and 10. Seven players started the day; four had spent the night in the living quarters and three in the prison.
- Morning: no match; each side talked about alliances for the day.
- **Main match:** Balance Mancala (`colour-mancala`), 7 players. Player A won; Player B scored lowest and was eliminated with no pieces left.
- **Prize mission:** the last one of the season, revealed after round 5: five times, move another player's marker onto the devil space so that the owner gains 5 or more points. Completed; cumulative prize 380 million won.
- **Hidden-stage reward used:** right after the housing announcement Player C claimed the living-quarters hidden-stage reward (10 pieces, claimable at any time). This changed the housing: Player A, the match winner, went to the prison.
- The episode 10 post-credits scene showed that on Day 3 Player C had told Player D and Player B what that reward was.
- **Living quarters:** Player C, Player D, Player E. **Prison:** Player A, Player F, Player G.
- After the match Player C gave Player D 2 pieces. In the prison the three redistributed their pieces (two went up by 1, Player A gave up 2). The living quarters watched the prison match on a relay screen.
- **Prison match:** Wall Go (`wall-territory`). Round 1: Player A won, survived and got 1 piece. Round 2: Player F beat Player G, who was eliminated.
- No new hidden stage was found this day.
- **Eliminated:** Player B (main match), Player G (prison match). Five players remained.
- **Pieces at the end of the day:** Player C 12, Player D 8, Player E 7, Player A 4, Player F 2.
- No other season-wide rule was introduced.

## Day 6
- Episode 11. Five players started the day: three from the living quarters and two from the prison.
- Pieces at the start: Player A 12, Player B 7, Player C 4, Player D 2, Player E 8.
- **Main match (semi-final):** Doubt and Bet (`colour-count-bluff`), 5 players. Player C was eliminated in round 19, Player D in round 42. Player E finished first and went straight to the final.
- **Prison match:** Equation Pyramid (`equation-target`), Player A against Player B. Player A won, became the second finalist and moved back to the living quarters. Player B was eliminated. No piece reward this time.
- **Hidden stages:** none found this day. **Prize mission:** none; cumulative prize 380 million won.
- A post-credits scene shows the two finalists talking after lights out.
- **Finalists:** Player E (14 pieces) and Player A (11 pieces).
- **Eliminated:** Player C and Player D (main match), Player B (prison match).
- No new season-wide rule was introduced.

## Day 7
- Episode 12, the final day. Two finalists: Player A (14 pieces) and Player B (11 pieces).
- Before the match the eliminated players returned and everyone had lunch together. In that talk it came out that Player B had understated to others the board sizes of the living-quarters hidden stage (a knight's tour): a guide footnote says they were 6x6, 7x7 and 8x8, not 5x5, 6x6 and 7x7.
- **Final rules:** three games, first to two wins; the finalist with more pieces gets a benefit in every game. Prize: 380 million won.
- Games in order:
  1. Big Small (`big-or-small`): Player B won, 21 chips to 20. Benefit: choice of role in round 1.
  2. Bagchal (`tiger-and-goat`): Player A won. Benefit: choice of first or second.
  3. Question and Truth (`question-and-truth`): Player B won. Benefit not broadcast.
- Each finalist practised Bagchal with one eliminated player before game 2.
- **Winner:** Player B, 2 to 1. **Runner-up:** Player A.
- No main or prison match, hidden stage, prize mission or housing change on this day.
