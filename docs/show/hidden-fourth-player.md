# Four Player Three in a Row on the show

How this game was played on The Devil's Plan. Our version: [rules page](../games/hidden-fourth-player.md).
Recorded from the NamuWiki episode guides (machine-translated), in our own words. Contestants
are Player A, B …; letters are assigned separately in each section. **Gaps** lists what the source
leaves open. Index: [On the show](README.md).

## Season 1, Day 6: prize match
- **Name in the source:** 4 Player 3 In a Row.
- **Players:** the three remaining players, Players A, B and C, and a guest player whose identity they did not know (the guest learned the rules an hour before).
- **Stakes:** ₩100 million for the prize money; 3 pieces to each round's winner; the two players with the most pieces after round 4 reached the final.

### Rules on the show
- 4 × 4 board. Each of the four gets 5 pieces of one colour: red, yellow, green or blue, which is also the turn order.
- All four play from separate rooms and cannot communicate; strategy time comes before the game.
- Colours change every round in a fixed rotation. With the guest as G the order red–yellow–green–blue was: round 1 G, B, A, C; round 2 A, C, G, B; round 3 C, G, B, A; round 4 B, A, C, G.
- On a turn, within 90 seconds, do one of two things:
  - place a new piece on any square, also on top of other pieces;
  - move one of your pieces that is on top of its square to one of the 8 neighbouring squares (one piece only).
- A stack is never higher than 3, by placing or moving. A player who can do neither passes.
- A round ends when, seen from above, one colour has 3 in a line (across, down or diagonal); that player wins.
- If two or more complete a line at once, neither gets the reward.
- 4 rounds. The mission fails if the guest wins any round, even jointly; it succeeds if the guest wins none.
- Equal pieces for a final place lead to a tie-break game.

### How it went
- Before the game: if the mission succeeded, A needed one round win to be sure of the final, B and C two. B and C had practised the evening before; A had played only a few times.
- B proposed fixing each player's opening move in advance for every round, so that the guest's first move would give away the guest's colour at once and the three could block it.
- C correctly worked out which colours A and B held.
- Rounds were short, about three moves per player.
- Round 1: won by B.
- Round 2: won by A.
- Round 3: won by A.
- Round 4: B misread the turn order at a decisive moment and the guest won.

### Result
- Mission failed; the prize money stayed at ₩250 million.
- Pieces: A 16 to 22, B 11 to 14, C 11 (no round won).
- A and B reached the final; C was eliminated (10th of the season).
- B apologised repeatedly for the mistake and later explained it was purely an error; C took the exit calmly.

### Differences from our version
- Show: 4 × 4 board; ours: 7 × 7.
- Show: 5 pieces each, stacks up to 3, pieces can move; ours: single stones, no movement.
- Show: a human guest among the four colours, to be identified; ours: the Shadow plays openly in its own colour and always last.
- Show: 4 short rounds, each ending at the first line, colours rotating; ours: one game, lines counted until the board is full.
- Show: separate rooms, 90 seconds per turn; ours: no communication rule, no timer.
- Show: pieces for round winners and a final place at stake, mission lost if the guest wins once; ours: points per own line plus a survival bonus.

### Gaps
- The move records and final boards are graphical and absent from the text.
- The round 1 winner is not stated in the text; it follows from the piece totals.
- The translation calls the guest "Spanish", apparently an error.
- The commentary mentions opening a first turn with three new pieces, which the listed rules do not include.
- It is unclear whether a round without any line can happen or how it would end.
