# The Virus Game on the show

How this game was played on The Devil's Plan. Our version: [rules page](../games/virus-game.md).
Recorded from the NamuWiki episode guides (machine-translated), in our own words. Contestants
are Player A, B …; letters are assigned separately in each section. **Gaps** lists what the source
leaves open. Index: [On the show](README.md).

## Season 1, Day 1: main match
- **Name in the source:** Virus Game (1st main match)
- **Players:** 12, each with one secret character and a unique number from 1 to 12, both drawn by lot. Terrorist team: 2 terrorists. Citizen team: 5 ordinary citizens (one of them secretly the antibody holder), 2 researchers, 1 reporter. Neutral: 1 fanatic, 1 investigator.
- **Stakes:** pieces by team and personal goal (below). The players with the most pieces then chose who went to prison.

### Rules on the show
- Setup: 60 minutes of free time after the rules; unique numbers are drawn, then each player picks a character in the dealer room. The antibody holder is whoever draws one particular citizen card (marked only by a different lettering); nobody is told, not even that player.
- Terrorists know each other; so do the two researchers. Ordinary citizens know nobody.
- Rounds last 30 minutes and repeat until a win condition is met. From round 2, each round opens with an announcement of the cure's progress, the deaths and the number of infected players.
- Abilities are secret missions performed physically; each player is told only their own:
  - Terrorist, contact: confirms the other terrorist's identity (the trigger was not shown).
  - Terrorist, infection (once per game, agreed by both): show the chosen player's number in front of a mirror in a certain room.
  - Terrorist, bullet (once per game each): point a finger gun at a player for 3 seconds; that player dies at once.
  - Terrorist, antidote (once per game each): stick the issued bandage on the back of the hand; cures one chosen player once.
  - Reporter (every round): use the phone in the kitchen/maintenance room and name a player; the phone tells that player's character. The antibody holder is reported as an ordinary citizen.
  - Researcher, manufacturing (each researcher, once per round): sit on a bench in the main hall holding hands with one other player for at least 5 seconds. Research advances only if that player is the antibody holder. After the first success, cure preparation is announced at the start of the next round; the second success ends the game with a citizen win.
  - Investigator, bullet (once every round): put a book upside down on the bookshelf in the storeroom; the living player whose number equals the book's volume number dies at once.
- Infection: starts from the player the terrorists pick and spreads along the numbers (12 and 1 are neighbours) in both directions, two new infections per round. Dead players' numbers are skipped. An antidote taken by a player in a round's infection path stops the spread in that direction. An infected player dies two rounds after infection.
- Antibody holder: if infected, recovers by themself after one round.
- Each player may test once per game, privately, at an inspection centre, whether they are infected.
- Dead players are isolated in the dealer room (they may talk among themselves only). Their name and character are announced; their number and whether they had antibodies are not.
- There is no vote.
- Winning and pieces:
  - Terrorists win when everyone except the antibody holder is dead (terrorists excluded): 4 pieces each.
  - Citizens win when both terrorists are dead or the cure is finished: 1 piece each. A surviving ordinary citizen: 1 extra. A surviving reporter: 1 extra. Researchers: 1 extra if the win came from the cure.
  - Investigator's goal: both terrorists dead. 4 pieces if reached in round 2, one fewer for each later round.
  - Fanatic's goal: to die. 3 pieces in round 1, 2 in rounds 2-3, 1 in round 4, none from round 5.

### How it went
- Characters: Player A and Player B were the terrorists, Player C the fanatic, Player D the investigator, Player E the reporter, Player F and Player G the researchers, Player H the antibody holder, and Players I, J, K and L ordinary citizens.
- Before the game: expecting terrorists to be at a disadvantage, many made pacts to tell each other their characters. Player A and Player C had agreed to share; Player C, although neutral, went on to work with both terrorists.
- Round 1: Player I proposed that everyone stay together in the main hall and do nothing, mainly to read who objected. Player B used the infection ability on Player I. Player E told several players she was the reporter. The researchers revealed themselves to Player A and both tried manufacturing with Player A (failed). The reporter checked Player A, learned she was a terrorist and told others. Player C had passed the reporter's identity to Player A, who shot Player E. Player C had also claimed to be the reporter, which was exposed by Player E's death. The investigator, who had not heard the reporter's claim in time, did not shoot.
- Round 2: 3 infected were announced. Player I tested positive and argued that Player A had started the infection. Seeing Player A cross her arms while being accused, Player D took this for a mission gesture and shot her; she was revealed as a terrorist. Player B shot researcher Player G. Researcher Player F tried manufacturing with Player J (failed). Suspicion of being the second terrorist fell heavily on Player C. 9 players left.
- Round 3: Player I died of the infection. Player F succeeded in manufacturing with Player H (1 of 2), but was already infected and the other researcher was dead, so the cure was out of reach. Player B falsely claimed to be the investigator; Player D then declared himself the real one. With about a minute left, Player D believed Player C and shot Player L, an ordinary citizen. 7 left.
- Round 4: Player K and Player F died of the infection. Unsure which of Player B and Player C was the fanatic and which the terrorist, Player D shot Player C, who was the fanatic and so met his goal. 4 left.
- Rounds 5-7: in round 5 Player B used the antidote on himself, Player D died of the infection and Player H recovered by antibody. Player J died of the infection in round 7.

### Result
- The terrorists won at the end of round 7, with only Player B and the antibody holder Player H left alive.
- Pieces gained: Player A +4 and Player B +4 (5 each), Player C +1 (fanatic, round 4). Everyone else stayed at 1; the surviving antibody holder got nothing extra.
- Players A and B, now holding the most pieces, chose the prisoners among the nine others (all but the fanatic, who nevertheless argued for sending weak players): one picked Player E, the other Player K. Nobody was eliminated.

### Differences from our version
- 12 players against 6-10 (page: 6).
- Our roles have no fanatic, antibody holder or shooting investigator; our officer only learns whether a met player is a terrorist, our journalist whom a player met.
- The show's infection spreads automatically along player numbers from one chosen start; ours needs each terrorist meeting.
- Show players die (infection, bullets) and leave play; ours has no deaths.
- The show has no vote; ours has accusations and a quarantine vote.
- Our researcher vaccinates by contact; the show's two researchers build a cure through the antibody holder, which wins outright.
- Show: open 30-minute rounds until a team wins (seven here); ours: 4 fixed rounds of contact, test, report, vote.
- Show terrorists win only if all non-antibody players die; ours if at least half of the others are infected.
- Show abilities are physical gestures others can notice; ours are private choices.
- Show pays pieces; ours points and one secret mission per player.

### Gaps
- The terrorists' contact trigger was not shown; one terrorist reportedly forgot it.
- The investigator's piece scale is garbled in a footnote; the main text gives 4 in round 2 and one fewer per round.
- The rules promise an extra piece to a surviving ordinary citizen "at the end of the game", but the surviving antibody holder received none; whether a team win was required is unclear.
- Timing of the antidote and how it interacts with the two-way spread are only roughly translated; so is who knew whose number.
- A passage on which bullets the terrorists still had in round 3 is unintelligible.
- The round 5 antidote entry is garbled; Player B using it on himself is the most likely reading.
