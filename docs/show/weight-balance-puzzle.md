# Scale Game on the show

How this game was played on The Devil's Plan. Our version: [rules page](../games/weight-balance-puzzle.md).
Recorded from the NamuWiki episode guides (machine-translated), in our own words. Contestants
are Player A, B …; letters are assigned separately in each section. **Gaps** lists what the source
leaves open. Index: [On the show](README.md).

## Season 1, Day 4: prize match
- **Name in the source:** "two arm scales" (Both Arms; rendered Scale Game in the rule header)
- **Players:** the 7 players not in prison, one cooperative team split into rooms of 3, 2 and 2
- **Stakes:** 100 million won added to the prize money; the player who gives the correct answer earns 2 pieces

### Rules on the show
- The teams of 3, 2 and 2 go to separate rooms (kitchen, maintenance room, dealer room). No contact between rooms until the game ends.
- Turns rotate between rooms: the kitchen has turns 1, 4 and 6; the maintenance room 2 and 5; the dealer room 3 and 7.
- Five minerals: red, yellow, green, blue, purple, with different weights. Each weighs a whole number of grams from 1 to 20. Each player gets 2 of each colour (10 in all). The wooden cubes only stand for the minerals.
- Minerals cannot be exchanged, moved to another player or destroyed.
- At the start the weight of one colour is revealed.
- Two scales: the main scale, which must end up balanced, and an auxiliary scale for working things out, which need not balance. A player uses them only on their own turn, either or both.
- On a turn the player places at least 2 minerals within 5 minutes. The scale readings are shown on monitors. Placed minerals stay and accumulate. The two sides of the main scale must not be made of the same minerals.
- If the main scale balances on your turn, you may stake 1 piece and state all the weights. You may also balance it without answering. A wrong answer loses the piece; the others learn only that it was wrong, and play goes on.
- A player with one mineral or none left can no longer place or answer. When nobody can place, the game ends.
- Success requires a balanced main scale and the correct weight of every mineral.
- 10 minutes of strategy time before the start.

### How it went
- Told in advance of the split, the players spread their pieces evenly over the three teams. In turn order: Players A, D and F in the kitchen (turns 1, 4, 6), B and E in the maintenance room (2, 5), C and G in the dealer room (3, 7). Each room had at least one strong calculator.
- Revealed at the start: yellow is the third heaviest and weighs 10 g.
- Round 1 (turns 1-7): comparisons on the auxiliary scale bounded the colours. By turn 5 purple was known to lie between three and four times red.
- Rounds 2 and 3 (turns 8-21): by turn 20 the full order and two weights were fixed: red 2 g and purple 7 g, below yellow, blue, green.
- Turn 23: B balanced the auxiliary scale for the first time, which let F work out blue 16 g and green 19 g. Later players added to it in ways that kept it level.
- F, the fastest calculator, was short of the minerals needed to balance the main scale and had to set it up in the hope that others would see the intent. At turn 27 F left it so that G could finish it.
- Turn 28: G added minerals but not the one red that would have balanced it. G and C had not worked out the weight of blue.
- Turn 29: only E could still place; the scale stayed unbalanced and the game ended.

### Result
- Mission failed. Prize money stayed at 150 million won.
- No piece changes. Pieces: C and E 4; B, D and G 3; A and F 2.
- Weights worked out: red 2, purple 7, yellow 10, blue 16, green 19 g.

### Differences from our version
- Show: 7 players in three isolated rooms; ours 1 to 2 players.
- Show: five colours with two copies per player, weights from 1 to 20, one weight given at the start; ours five single blocks, weights 1 to 9, none given.
- Show: two cumulative scales, minerals limited and never taken back; ours one scale, reloaded each time, 12 weighings.
- Show: no counterweights; ours 0 to 20 g per pan.
- Show: the goal is to balance the main scale and then answer, staking a piece; ours answers when the team chooses, each player in secret.
- Show: all-or-nothing prize; ours points per block and a bonus for unused weighings.

### Gaps
- The number of minerals per player is read from the remaining-mineral tables; the rule line is garbled.
- The wording of the restriction on the main scale is garbled; the reading above may be wrong.
- Whether a player with only 1 piece could answer was not said.
- Several turns were not aired and are reconstructed by the guide. One comparison sign (turn 28) contradicts the listed weights, and some of its early deduction notes are garbled.
- After the game G is reported saying no red was left, while the guide's table shows G still holding one.
