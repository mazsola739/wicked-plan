## Season 2 (Death Room), Day 3: prison match
- **Name in the source:** Sniper Hold'em
- **Players:** 5 from the prison quarters (Players A to E), each for themselves
- **Stakes:** reach 75 chips to be safe; the last player left is eliminated. No pieces were awarded.

### Rules on the show
- Deck: four sets of number cards 1 to 10. Each player starts with 60 chips.
- A card draw sets the first player; play goes clockwise.
- Each round: everyone posts 1 chip and gets 2 private cards. The dealer opens 2 shared cards, then there is a first betting round. Then 2 more shared cards are opened and there is a second betting round.
- Nobody can bet more than the chip count of the player with the fewest chips.
- A hand is the best combination from the 4 shared and 2 private cards. Ranks from high to low: four of a kind, full house, straight (five in a row), triple, two pair, one pair, high card. No flush.
- Snipe: before private cards are shown, players in turn from the first player may each name one hand they want to shoot down, with its number (for example a straight up to 8, or two pair headed by 7). A full house is named by the number of its triple.
- After all snipes the private cards are revealed. The best hand takes the bets, but any sniped hand drops to the bottom. Different sniped hands all share last place.
- Ties: same rank, the higher numbers in the combination win; then the higher private cards decide.
- If the private cards match completely, or every player's hand was sniped, the round is a draw and the bets are shared; leftover chips go one at a time in turn order.
- A player who reaches 75 chips or more pays 75 to the dealer and is safe. Chips above 75 are handed to other players as the leaver likes, and any player with no chips must get at least one.
- The match continues until only one player has not become safe.

### How it went
- It ran 68 rounds, more than three hours, past midnight.
- Rounds 1 to 7 (five players): C folded early to watch the others. In round 3, A sniped B's straight and won. In round 7, A reached 85 chips, left first and gave 7 spare chips to B and 3 to C.
- Round 8 (four players): a large pot with pairs on the table. B sniped a full house, C sniped C's own two pair hoping to force a draw, and E also sniped a full house. B's two pair headed by 10 survived and B reached 100. At C's request to keep the game fair, B split the 25 spare chips: 11 to D, 13 to E, 1 to C.
- Three players (D 47, C 56, E 47): a long middle phase with many small pots. E, new to poker, landed several accurate snipes; C's hidden pocket pair in round 30 made a triple that was hard to guess and won a big pot.
- Round 40: C reached 78 chips, left third and gave the 3 spare chips to D. D had 24, E 51.
- Heads-up (rounds 41 to 68): D fell as low as about 10 chips and survived with repeated all-ins. In round 51 both sniped each other's hand and the round was drawn. D once chose not to snipe, to give away nothing. In round 60, with no snipes and both holding a full house, D won on the tie-break and took the lead. In round 66 E's snipe missed while D's hit. In round 68 D's pair of 9s survived, taking D to 75 and E to 0.

### Result
- Safe, in order: A, B, C, D. Eliminated: E.
- No pieces were given for this match.

### Differences from our version
- Show: 60 chips and play until players reach 75, leaving one by one; ours: 25 chips, 6 hands, most chips wins.
- Show: two betting rounds with 2 shared cards opened before each; ours: all 4 shared cards at once and one betting round.
- Show: free bet sizes up to all-in; ours: raise by 2, at most 3 raises.
- Show: a snipe names a rank and its number; ours names only a rank.
- Show: sniping goes in turn; ours is secret and revealed together.
- Show: a sniped hand drops to last, and if all are sniped the bets are shared; ours knocks the hand out and carries the pot over.
- Show has no flush or straight flush; ours has colours with both.
- Show: surplus chips are given to others on leaving; ours has no such rule.

### Gaps
- Many rounds are summarised or have cards marked as not shown, and the two-player card tables are garbled in translation.
- Skipping a snipe is not in the stated rules, though players did it.
- That a hand is exactly five of the six cards is inferred from the examples.
- The exact wording of the tie-break and of the forced-chip rule for players at 0 is unclear in translation.

## Season 2 (Death Room), game-rules page
Source: the game-rules page has no block for this game; these points come from the rule block and a footnote on the Day 3 page.

### Rules added
- Sniped hands are not compared with one another, even when their ranks differ: they share last place and the tie-breaks do not apply to them.

### Corrections to the day record
- Tie-break order for hands of equal rank has three steps, not two: first the higher numbers forming the combination, then the higher other card in the hand, then the higher private cards. Only when the private cards match exactly, or every player's hand is sniped, is the round drawn.

### Gaps
- Not sniping is still absent from the stated rules; the guide notes this too.
- The tie-break wording is rough in translation.
