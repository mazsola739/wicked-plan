# Treasure Island on the show

How this game was played on The Devil's Plan. Our version: [rules page](../games/treasure-island.md).
Recorded from the NamuWiki episode guides (machine-translated), in our own words. Contestants
are Player A, B …; letters are assigned separately in each section. **Gaps** lists what the source
leaves open. Index: [On the show](README.md).
A **game-rules page** section comes after the day sections: where it adds to or corrects

## Season 2 (Death Room), Day 4: main match
- **Name in the source:** Treasure Island
- **Players:** 9, each scoring alone, split into two alliances: five from the living quarters (Players A to E) and four from prison (Players F to I)
- **Stakes:** pieces won or lost by final victory points; a special piece reward for finding the treasure. Prize mission: 100 million won if only one player is eliminated when the match ends.

### Rules on the show
Board and boxes
- A three-dimensional 9 x 9 board with land squares, water squares, fences and walls, and one start point.
- Ten boxes are on land squares; one is the treasure chest, the other nine hold clues. Players get a small printed plan before the game. It shows only nine boxes clearly: the brown treasure box is drawn in the same colour as the land. The red connection dots are not on the plan and can be seen only on the real board.
- Box values: green and sky blue 5; yellow, white and brown 8; pink and blue 12; red, orange and purple 15.

Rounds
- 9 rounds: rounds 1, 2, 3, 5 and 7 are arrow auctions; rounds 4, 6, 8 and 9 are searches.
- Each player starts with 20 auction chips (not transferable) and one basic arrow.

Auction round
- A set of arrows (single arrows and bundles of two) is put up. In an isolated booth each player gives chips to the dealer; at least 1 chip to take part. Amounts are secret and spent.
- Players pick an arrow or bundle in order of chips bid. Ties go by number of pieces (more first, judging by the results), then by lot.
- A player with no chips left takes no part.
- Arrows may not be damaged or bent.

Search round
- 40 minutes; players enter the dealer room freely, at most 3 minutes per visit, and queue first come, first served.
- Arrows are laid from the start point. Every arrow must begin and end on a red dot (the arrow's dots on the board's dots) and may be chained end to end with no limit, as one continuous path. Apart from its two ends an arrow should not touch anything on the way.
- Arrows cannot be given or traded, but a player may borrow at most one from another by agreement.
- An arrow path that reaches a box lets the player open it, or the path may go on past it. A player cannot open the same box twice.
- The arrows used to open a box are taken back for the rest of that round and returned at the next one; unused arrows may be used for another path from the start.
- The first player ever to open a box gets its full value; every later opener gets 5.
- At any time a player may pay 1 piece to read a box's clue, without scoring that box.

End
- The game ends after round 9, or as soon as someone opens the treasure chest. Its finder gets a special reward, announced on opening: take 4 pieces from other players.
- By final points: 31 or more gives +1 piece, 41 or more +2; 20 or fewer costs 1 piece, and a lower band (apparently 10 or fewer) costs 2.

Arrow types (displacement in squares)
- Corner (1,1); lightning (3,1); S shape (2,0); hook (2,1); U shape (1,1); snake (4,0); large (4,3). The guide treats the corner and the large arrow as basic ones. The large arrow needs more room than its displacement suggests, as it goes out and comes back.

Clues in the boxes
- Sky blue: the red and orange boxes hold no treasure, but learning to reach them helps later.
- Green: points to finding where each colour's box stands.
- White: reaching red, orange and purple needs creative use of arrows.
- Pink, blue, yellow: how to reach red, orange and purple respectively.
- Purple: the treasure square touches two fences.
- Red: the large arrow is needed to reach the treasure.
- Orange: the same arrow can be used twice on the way to the treasure.
- Brown: the treasure chest itself.

### How it went
- Planning: the five-player alliance worked out that the unseen tenth box was probably the treasure and that a variety of arrows mattered, because used arrows come back. Both sides asked the dealer about turning arrows, leaving the board and crossing fences, and only heard the red-dot rule repeated.
- Round 1 (auction): mostly 1 or 2 chips; F bid 3 for first pick and took the snake. The five bought up the lightning arrows after G took a U-shaped one.
- Round 2: B went all in to 16 chips for first pick, then regretted it on learning an ally was already providing what was needed. F was outbid for the wanted lightning arrow.
- Round 3: the four poured in chips (H, G and I bid 17, leaving 1, 0 and 0) to get ahead for the first search. The five also assigned first boxes to open.
- Round 4 (search): the real board's walls and fences surprised the four. H opened yellow first (and sky blue), G opened pink first, A opened blue first. The five got the red and orange clues through the pink and blue boxes; the four realised arrows need not lie along the grid, and that spending all chips had been a mistake.
- Round 5 (auction): the five bid calmly, knowing the others were nearly out of chips. F bid 8 and took first pick. Ideas for the red, purple and orange boxes went through water and over walls.
- Round 6: D opened orange after turning the path to clear a fence. The planned red and purple routes failed (the wall was too high; dots exist only on land). E opened white and yellow; the yellow clue led D to a purple route with an arrow turned upside down, and C worked out a red route between wall and water.
- Round 7: the last auction; H, G and I had no chips. B refused H a blue-box hint for fear that I, who held matching arrows, would profit.
- Round 8: C opened red and B purple. That gave the five the treasure square, and A confirmed in the dealer room a route using two large arrows. G's purple route failed. I held two large arrows but did not know how to use them.
- Round 9: the five collected remaining points while the four tried to keep I above the penalty line. C offered F help to avoid a penalty; F declined. A dispute broke out over the queue, A let F and G go first, then opened the treasure chest and ended the game.

### Result
- Final points: B, C, D 45; A 40; E 33; G 32; F 25; H 23; I 20.
- Point rewards: B, C, D +2 pieces; A, E, G +1; I -1, down to 0 and eliminated.
- A's treasure reward: A chose to avoid eliminating anyone further and, persuaded by C to take only from the prison players, took 1 from F, 1 from G and 2 from H.
- Pieces after the match: E 13, A 11, B 8, C 8, D 8, F 3, G 2, H 2, I 0.
- Living quarters: A, E and two of B, C, D; E, holding the most pieces, sent D to prison. Prize mission completed (cumulative 280 million won).

### Differences from our version
- Show: a 3D board with water, fences and walls, and a spatial puzzle of laying shaped arrows from a start point; ours: an open 9 x 9 grid where a box shows a direction arrow toward the treasure.
- Show: auctions sell arrows (movement pieces), with picking order for all bidders; ours: 5 auctions for the right to open one box, highest bid only (ties all pay).
- Show: 20 chips and secret spent bids; ours: 100 coins, all bids shown afterwards, and kept coins score.
- Show: 10 boxes with their own points, first opener gets full value; ours: boxes score nothing.
- Show: 4 search rounds and the game ends when the chest is opened; ours: one dig per player, scored by distance.
- Show: piece rewards and penalties and the right to take 4 pieces; ours: points only.
### Gaps
- Arrow shapes, box positions on the plan and the box-opening routes are mostly pictures; the arrow names are the guide's own.
- The lower penalty band is garbled in translation (read as 10 or fewer for 2 pieces).
- The guide's stated number of arrows per auction (at least 11 to 13) does not match the 9 or 10 lots it lists; the round 7 list is identical to round 5's.
- Some bids by Player C and several round 3 picks were not broadcast.
- Player A's running score is inconsistent between search rounds in the guide; the final total is consistent.

## Season 2 (Death Room), game-rules page
Source: the game-rules page has no block for this game; these points come from folded text, the board legend and the search logs of the Day 4 page. Coordinates are the guide's own (columns A to I, rows 1 to 9).

### Rules added
- Treasure reward: the 4 pieces may all come from one player or be split, 1 to 3 each, among several. It is settled after the piece changes from victory points.
- Each land square has one red dot, in the exact centre of its top. The dots appear only on the real board in the dealer room, not on the smaller printed plan.
- Start: C2. Boxes: blue A1, sky blue A2, orange I1, red G5, green C6, purple A8, yellow D8, white F8, pink G8, brown (treasure) H7.
- Arrows may be turned to any angle that joins two dots. Logged routes:
  - treasure: large arrow C2 to H2, large arrow H2 to H7
  - orange: large arrow to H2, corner arrow to I1
  - purple: lightning to B5, lightning to A8
  - pink: snake to C6, lightning to F7, corner arrow to G8
  - blue: hook to D4, lightning to A3, corner arrow to B2, corner arrow to A1
