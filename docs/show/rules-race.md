# Rules Race on the show

How this game was played on The Devil's Plan. Our version: [rules page](../games/rules-race.md).
Recorded from the NamuWiki episode guides (machine-translated), in our own words. Contestants
are Player A, B …; letters are assigned separately in each section. **Gaps** lists what the source
leaves open. Index: [On the show](README.md).

## Season 1, Day 2: main match
- **Name in the source:** Rule Race (2nd main match)
- **Players:** 12, each for themself; in practice two alliances, a minority of four (the piece-rich players) and a majority of eight
- **Stakes:** pieces by finishing place: 1st +3, 2nd +2, 3rd +1, 4th-9th nothing, 10th -1, 11th -2, 12th -5. A player left with no pieces is eliminated.

### Rules on the show
Personal rule (secret)
- Built from lettered blocks in nine colours, one block per colour: a condition part and a result part.
- Blocks: place/rank adjectives (in the same space as me, in prison, next after me in turn order, leading, last; one set for each part); subjects (condition: "the player" or "every player"; result: "the player" or "I"); things (die roll, 1 escape ticket, special die, personal rules, group rules, die faces 1 / 2 / 3 / prison / escape); targets (a number of squares such as 10 or 20, 1 escape ticket, nearest office, nearest space of a given colour); verbs (comes up, is trapped, escapes, is discarded, is used, is moved by; move forward, move back, go to, obtain, discard, play with the special die).
- Allowed conditions: a die face comes up; someone is jailed or escapes; the special die is used; an escape ticket is used or discarded; someone is moved by a personal or group rule. Allowed results: move forward or back; gain or discard a ticket; go to the nearest space of a kind; go to prison; a special-die play.
- It must be grammatical, name its target exactly, not contradict itself, and not use the same subject block in both parts (naming the same player by different words is possible).
- 120 minutes to write it, then register it in the dealer room. Only its effect is seen when it fires.
- Everyone has a full block set, so identical rules are possible.

Order
- Fewest letters in the personal rule plays first; ties by rock-paper-scissors.

Dice and movement
- Normal die: one face 1, two faces 2, one face 3, one prison, one escape.
- Special die (only when a rule allows it): one face 1, two faces 2, three faces 3.
- All start on the start space. The track runs through squares 1-19 and then the goal; offices on squares 4, 8, 11 and 17; overshooting the goal still finishes.
- Rolling the normal die ends the turn, so group rules and tickets must be used before it.

Prison
- Prison face: mark your square and move to prison. A rule can also send a player there, even from the start; nobody can be sent back to the start once they have left it.
- On later turns, roll to try to get out (the escape face) or hand in 1 escape ticket; after getting out you may roll at once and move.
- The escape face does nothing for a free player.

Offices
- Landing on an office by a die move lets you choose one: (1) 1 escape ticket, (2) roll again, (3) register a group rule or change one. Arriving by a rule's movement, or staying there after jail, gives nothing.

Group rules (public, for everyone)
- A sentence of one phrase from each of three colour groups. Several can exist, and an existing one can be edited by swapping phrases (resubmitting it unchanged is allowed).
- Phrase group 1: all players on their turn; jailed players at any time; the leading player on their turn.
- Phrase group 2 (condition or price): not going to prison on a prison roll; moving 1, 2 or 3 on that roll; another player being sent to prison; pay 1 piece; go back 2; discard an escape ticket; give up the die roll.
- Phrase group 3 (effect): advance 1; get 1 ticket; give 1 ticket to a chosen player; roll again; move to the square of the player just ahead; go back 1; roll the special die; move another player back 1.

General
- Rules can fire any number of times when their conditions hold. If one event satisfies several personal rules, they apply in turn order. "Leading" and "last" mean a single player, so a tie counts for nobody.
- A player who finishes no longer triggers their rule and their tickets are returned.
- The game ends when the ranking is complete.

### How it went
Turn order and personal rules (paraphrased; "owner gets a ticket" means the rule's writer gains 1 escape ticket):
- Player A: the leader is jailed, owner gets a ticket.
- Player B: the leader rolls a 2, owner gets a ticket.
- Player C: a prisoner escapes, owner gets a ticket.
- Player D: the last player is moved by a personal rule, owner gets a ticket.
- Player E: someone on the owner's square is jailed, owner gets a ticket.
- Player F: the leader is jailed by a die roll, owner gets a ticket.
- Player G: a prisoner uses a ticket, owner gets a ticket.
- Player H: the leader is moved by a group rule, the last player goes to prison.
- Player I: the last player is moved by a personal rule, the leader goes to prison.
- Player J: the same rule as Player I.
- Player K: the same rule as Player G.
- Player L: the leader uses a ticket, whoever shares the owner's square goes to prison.

Course:
- Minority: Players H, I, J and K. Majority: the other eight, whose plan was personal rules that earn escape tickets and group rules that pass them around. The minority guessed that the majority's rules caused movement and wrote counters to that; the guess was wrong, so the minority's three prison rules (H, I, J) never fired.
- Rounds 1-3: the first group rules appeared at offices (give up the roll to give away a ticket; pay a piece to move up or to roll the special die). Player C's rule paid out first, when another player escaped in round 3.
- Rounds 4-6: escapes let Players C, G and K collect tickets. The majority used its tickets to get members out of prison, often at the cost of their own moves. Player J switched a transfer rule to "discard a ticket to give a ticket", letting the minority share Player K's tickets without paying pieces or losing a roll.
- Round 7: both sides rewrote the group rules back and forth; Player I restored "pay a piece for the special die", and Player J paid to catch up.
- Round 8: Player D introduced "discard a ticket to push another player back 1", used on Player J. Player I set up "discard a ticket to roll the special die"; Player K spent tickets on three special rolls in one turn and finished first. The minority thereby lost its main source of tickets.
- Rounds 9-10: Player G used the new rules and offices to climb out of the bottom half; Player H changed the push-back rule at an office, Player L restored it, and the group rules did not change again. Player B left the start only around round 10.
- Rounds 11-20: much was cut. The majority repeatedly pushed Players H and I back. Finishes: Player L (round 13), F (14), D (16), C (17), G (18), A (20).
- Rounds 21-30: rules mattered little and the dice decided: Player B finished in round 26, J in 27, E in 28, I in 30; Player H was last.

### Result
- Order: 1 Player K, 2 Player L, 3 Player F, 4 Player D, 5 Player C, 6 Player G, 7 Player A, 8 Player B, 9 Player J, 10 Player E, 11 Player I, 12 Player H.
- Pieces after the match: Player K 5 (+3), L 3 (+2), F 2 (+1); J 2 (spent 2 in play); E 1 (-1); I 2 (spent 1, lost 2); H 0 (had 4, owed 5), the season's first elimination.
- The winner chose two prisoners from the majority side: Players G and B.

### Differences from our version
- 12 players against 4-8 (page: 4).
- Die: show 1, 2, 2, 3, prison, escape, plus a special die; ours 1, 2, 3, 3, prison, escape only.
- Track: show 19 squares and a goal with offices, played until everyone but the last has finished (30 rounds); ours squares 0-18, ends at the first finisher or after 15 rounds.
- Personal rules: show free sentences from many blocks (own or others' events, ticket use, rule movement); ours one of 5 conditions on another player's roll and one of 5 effects.
- Group rules: show built from three phrases and rewritten at offices, often as paid actions; ours four fixed switches voted on every 3rd round.
- Escape tickets: show unlimited and transferable; ours at most one card.
- Prison: show only the escape face or a ticket frees you (then you roll again); ours releases after two turns unless Strict prison is on, and an escape roll moves 2.
- Escape face while free: nothing on the show; ours gives a card or moves 1.
- Show turn order follows rule length; ours seat order.
- Show costs and pays pieces, with elimination; ours ranks by square and pays points.

### Gaps
- The block lists are machine-translated: the exact wording of several blocks, and the "play with the special die" result, are uncertain.
- The second group-rule phrase group reads as conditions or prices; the exact wording of the prison and number-roll phrases, and how they worked, is unclear.
- How many group rules could be active at once, and how an edit chose which one to replace, are not spelled out.
- Square numbering is the guide's own, used to describe progress.
- Rounds 11-16 were largely cut from the broadcast; several rolls are unknown.
- The guide's two piece histories disagree for J (the amounts add to 3 at day's end, one table says 2).
- Whether the winner was barred from picking the previous day's prisoner or simply chose not to is unclear.
