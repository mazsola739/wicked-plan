# Fragments of Memory on the show

How this game was played on The Devil's Plan. Our version: [rules page](../games/memory-scene-quiz.md).
Recorded from the NamuWiki episode guides (machine-translated), in our own words. Contestants
are Player A, B …; letters are assigned separately in each section. **Gaps** lists what the source

## Season 1, Day 2: prize match
- **Name in the source:** Fragments of Memory (2nd prize match; also translated "Sculpture of Memory")
- **Players:** the 9 players still in the living quarters (2 in prison, 1 eliminated)
- **Stakes:** 50 million won for the prize pot on success; on failure the player who answered wrongly gives back 1 piece

### Rules on the show
- A picture full of details is shown for 20 minutes on a large screen (physical panels too) in the central hall. Players may not leave the hall or take notes; writing tools are taken away. A practice example is shown beforehand.
- Then all go to the kitchen and agree their order freely; after 5 minutes the questions begin.
- 10 questions about the picture, getting harder. They are revealed one at a time to the player who comes out to the hall; the others do not see them.
- The challenger keeps answering, or may pass. A player who passes is out of the match for good and the next player comes out.
- One wrong answer ends the match as a failure; so does everyone passing.

### How it went
- Some had guessed a memory game when they saw the screen.
- Player A went first and passed on the first question, believing she could skip easy ones and take harder ones later; passing removed her.
- Player B came second and answered all 10 questions.
- Question types: names on signs and of people in the picture, a uniform's colour, the food being delivered, objects people held, the current date in the picture (it had to be worked out from a calendar, a long operation and the time of night; Player C's objection earlier had steered the group to the right reading), and which eye a patient's patch covered.

### Result
- Success: 50 million won. No personal piece reward.

### Differences from our version
- 9 players against 1-4.
- Show: one detailed illustrated scene studied for 20 minutes; ours: 8 shaped, coloured objects on a grid, studied until all are ready.
- Show: one challenger at a time until they pass, order chosen by the group; ours: turns rotate each question.
- Show allows passing, which removes the player; ours has no pass.
- Show answers are spoken freely; ours picks one of 4.
- Show hides questions from the others; ours shows everyone.
- Show penalises the wrong answerer a piece and pays prize money; ours only scores points.
