## Season 3 (Dead Again), Day 6: main match
- **Name in the source:** HIT, STAY (6th main match)
- **Players:** 5, each for themselves
- **Stakes:** the first player to go broke is eliminated from the show; the last player with chips goes straight to the final; the other three go to the prison match

### Rules on the show
- **First player:** drawn by lot; play is clockwise, and each new round the first player moves one seat to the left.
- **35 number cards:** one 7, two 6, three 5, four 4, five 3, six 2, seven 1, seven -1.
- **Chips:** 30 each, plus 1 for every piece held.
- **Ante:** 1 chip from everyone each round; it rises by 1 every 10 rounds.
- **Limit:** after the ante the dealer rolls an 8-sided die numbered 5 to 12; the result is this round's limit for card sums.
- **Deal:** 3 cards face down to each player, who then shows one of them.
- **First betting round**, starting with the first player: call, raise or fold, until the bets are equal. No bet may exceed the chips of the poorest player (no side pots).
- **Hit or stay:** players still in decide in turn, from the first player. Hit: one more card, dealt face up. Stay: no more cards. Repeated until the cards run out or everyone stays.
- **Second betting round**, same way.
- **Showdown:** all cards shown. A sum over the limit ranks last. Among sums within the limit the closest wins the pot; equal sums: more cards wins; equal card count too: draw. If everyone is over, draw.
- In a draw the tied players split the pot; leftover chips go to the one nearer the first player.
- The first player to reach 0 chips is eliminated. Play goes on until only one player has chips.

### How it went
**Before the game:** a last choice between "public good" (if at least 3 of 5 chose it, a prize-money token adding 100 million, making 300 million) and "self-interest" (each chooser gets 10 chips). Players who had chosen the public good on Day 1 wanted the two who had chosen self-interest to commit first; those two would not. All five took the 10 chips, so the prize stayed at 200 million.

Starting stacks by seat: A 45, B 40, C 44, D 44, E 48.

**Round 1** (limit 8). Hands: A (-1, 3, 1), B (7, 1, 2), C (1, 4, -1), D (-1, 4, 2), E (-1, 5, 4); shown cards 1, 2, -1, 2, 4. A bet 3, B and C folded, D called, E raised to 10. A then raised heavily, D folded, E put in the rest of A's stack: A was all in, pot 96. A hit four times (6, 2, 3, 5) and ended over the limit; E stayed on exactly 8 and won. A was eliminated in the first round. Stacks: B 39, C 43, D 40, E 99.

**Round 2** (limit 9). Hands: B (-1, -1, 1), C (4, 3, 1), D (3, 4, 2), E (4, 5, 3). D, holding exactly 9, bet 30 and the others gave up. Stacks: B 36, C 40, D 47, E 98.

**Round 3** (limit 11), order C, D, E, B. Hands: C (1, 3, 3), D (-1, 4, 2), E (-1, 5, 3), B (-1, 3, 4). D folded; C, E and B bet 1 each (pot 7). Hits: C one card (7, over), E one card (3, total 10), B four cards (-1, 1, 1, 6, over). C and E bet 6 each, B folded (pot 19). E won. Stacks: B 34, C 32, D 46, E 109.

**Later rounds:** B then lost everything, falling for a bluff by C. D, whose habit of betting big only on strong hands had been read, ran out too. In the last duel C kept reading E's tendencies until E faked a displeased look and bluffed back, which cost C many chips. Finally E, holding the better hand, bet big to draw C all in, and C went broke. The headings put the three-player stage at rounds 11 to 15 and the last duel in round 16.

### Result
1. E: to the final
2. C: prison
3. D: prison
4. B: prison
5. A: eliminated
