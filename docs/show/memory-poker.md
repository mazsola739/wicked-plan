## Season 3 (Dead Again), Day 1: mini-game 4 of the main match (Either/Or)
- **Name in the source:** Memory Poker
- **Players:** the bottom two of the 16 as challengers, against higher-ranked players in one-on-one duels
- **Stakes:** rank positions; whoever stood 15th and 16th at the end was eliminated

### Rules on the show
- 16 cards from a 52-card deck are shown at random for 20 seconds and must be memorised. Then 3 shared cards are revealed.
- Each duellist picks 2 of the 16 memorised cards by placing pieces on those positions of a personal board (positions A-P, a 4 x 4 grid). Both may pick the same cards. Picks are revealed when both are done.
- The better poker hand (the 2 picks plus the 3 shared cards, Hold'em ranking) wins. Equal combinations: higher ranks in the combination win, then the remaining cards. Suits are not compared.
- The 15th-placed player challenges first, then the 16th.
- A tenth choice, made by all players just before, fixes the format:
  - **Nomination** (8 or more votes): the challenger names any higher-ranked player, never the same one twice. A winning challenger moves up one rank; the loser drops one rank, swapping with the player below. A nominated player who wins gets 1 piece.
  - **Ladder** (9 or more votes): the challenger plays the player directly above, moves up one on a win and carries on to the next one above.
- A challenger's run ends on a defeat. If the match leaves someone new in 15th or 16th, that player plays next; someone who had been there before does not get a second run. With no newcomer the game ends.

### How it went
The ladder was chosen. Starting places from 10th to 16th: Players A, B, C, D, E, F, G.

| Duel | Challenger's hand | Opponent's hand | Winner |
|---|---|---|---|
| F vs E, deal 1 | three queens | three queens | draw (both had the best hand) |
| F vs E, deal 2 | four aces (best possible) | ace-high full house | F; F to 14th |
| F vs D | ace high | two pair | D; F's run ends |
| G vs E (now 15th) | two pair | three sevens | E; G is certain to be out |
| E vs F (14th) | two pair | pair of sixes | E; F back to 15th |
| E vs D | ten-high full house (best possible) | two pair | E |
| E vs C | pair of kings | nine high | E |
| E vs B | two pair, aces up | pair of aces | E |
| E vs A | pair of fours | straight | A; the game ends |

- E said to have memorised the cards by position, like a keypad, and sharpened with each duel.
- Several deals ended with neither side near the best possible hand.

### Result
- Final places 10-16: A, E, B, C, D, F, G. Places 1-9 never had to play.
- Records (draws not counted): E 5 wins 2 losses; F 1 win 2 losses; D 1 win 1 loss; A 1 win; B, C and G 1 loss each.
- F (15th) and G (16th) were eliminated.

### Differences from our version
- Show: one-on-one duels on a rank ladder that decides elimination. Ours: 3 rounds, up to 4 seats, points per hand.
- Show: both duellists choose independently and may take the same cards. Ours: cards are taken in snake order and are then gone.
- Show: 20 seconds to memorise. Ours: until everyone taps Ready.
- Show compares hands only; ours gives fixed points per hand type plus a best-hand bonus.

### Gaps
- Any time limit for picking is not given.
- The sentence on when a run ends is garbled (it mentions two defeats); the played results fit "ends on a defeat".
- Replaying a draw is not written as a rule; it is what happened.
