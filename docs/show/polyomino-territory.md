# Laying Grass on the show

How this game was played on The Devil's Plan. Our version: [rules page](../games/polyomino-territory.md).
Recorded from the NamuWiki episode guides (machine-translated), in our own words. Contestants
are Player A, B …; letters are assigned separately in each section. **Gaps** lists what the source
leaves open. Index: [On the show](README.md).

## Season 1, Day 5: main match
- **Name in the source:** Laying Grass (machine translation also "Eating ground" / "Eating Land").
- **Players:** 9, each for themselves. In turn order (drawn by lot): Players A, B, C, D, E, F, G, H, I, starting with 2, 3, 2, 3, 3, 3, 3, 4 and 1 pieces.
- **Stakes:** pieces by the size of one's largest square, plus a winner's bonus. A player at 0 pieces is eliminated. This match also decided who went to prison; from Day 6 there was no prison penalty for the lowest ranked.

### Rules on the show
- Board of 30 × 30 squares (900).
- Each player gets one 1 × 1 base tile and one tile change coupon. 9 rounds, one grass tile per player per round.
- 96 grass tiles of various shapes lie in a fixed queue set in advance. On your turn you take the next tile.
- Coupon: instead of the next tile, pick one of the following five. The queue then continues after the chosen tile; skipped tiles come up again only after the queue has run through to the end.
- One coupon costs 1 piece. Coupons may be given or traded between players.
- A tile may be turned (90, 180, 270 degrees) and flipped.
- The first tile must share at least one edge with your base tile; later tiles must join your own territory.
- A tile may not overlap a tile, leave the board, or share an edge with an opponent's territory (corner contact is allowed). Placed tiles never move.
- Time limit 2 minutes per placement; it starts again when a coupon is used.
- A tile not placed in time, or with no legal place, goes back to the dealer and is out of the game.
- **Special squares:** surrounding one with your own territory on its four sides makes it yours and triggers it. Covering it with a tile instead is allowed and does not trigger it. Each works once.
  - Coupon square: gain 1 coupon.
  - Piece square: gain 1 piece.
  - Stone square: at once, put a 1 × 1 stone on any empty square.
  - Robbery square: at once, take one grass tile from an opponent's territory and place it as part of your own.
- **Stones:** grass tiles may not be placed against a stone. On your turn you may pay 1 piece to remove a stone. A stone never counts as territory, even when surrounded.
- **After round 9:** players may buy a 1 × 1 grass tile for 1 piece and place it on an empty square of their choice.
- **Scoring** by the largest square in one's territory: 2 × 2: −3 pieces; 3 × 3: −2; 4 × 4: −1; 5 × 5: 0; 6 × 6: +1; 7 × 7: +2; 8 × 8: +3.
- The largest square also wins 3 bonus pieces. Tied winners split them; if they cannot be split evenly, nobody gets them.

### How it went
- Before the start A offered C an alliance, to repay earlier help; C accepted.
- **Rounds 1–3:** the first four players began by the corner coupon squares; F reached toward a robbery and a coupon square; G started by the central special squares. A used a coupon (tile 22) to head for two adjacent robbery squares and suggested boxing G in. G warned that an attack would be answered with a stone. B was the first to secure a special square. By round 3, B, D, E, G and H had 3 × 3. A 30-minute free talk followed.
- **Round 4:** A placed to keep G away from the stone squares. D switched, at H's request, to building a 4 × 4 (the first), which also got in the way of F's robbery.
- **Round 5:** C spent a coupon to reach a robbery square and took a tile from G; G announced retaliation. B, C, D, H and I had 4 × 4.
- **Round 6:** A reached a robbery square with a diagonally running tile and took another of G's tiles, also hindering G's stone square; C blocked that stone square further. E, advised by G, used a coupon to keep a robbery square open and to block C, who had to grow along the board's edge. G collected a piece square. E made the first 5 × 5. In the next 30-minute talk F sought help against a tile in H's area through B's possible robbery, and B gave F a coupon (F said in an interview that the weak image was deliberate).
- **Round 7:** the queue passed tile 96 and began again with the skipped tiles. G took the plain next tile after working out coupons and turn order. E, F and H stood at 5 × 5.
- **Round 8:** F completed the first 6 × 6. H could not place and lost a tile.
- **Round 9:** A and H could not place. H noticed that D could still reach a robbery square and paid for D's coupon, hoping D would break E's square and leave F as sole winner. E and F finished at 6 × 6. E bought a coupon for I so that I could make 6 × 6, take the reward and stay out of prison; I accepted and promised a piece in return.
- **Single-tile purchases:** C bought one and reached 5 × 5. D bought one, triggered the robbery square and, to general surprise, took a tile from C's area. It did not enlarge D's square; it cut C back to 4 × 4 and sent C out. E, F and I stayed tied, so H's plan failed. In an interview D said the choice settled who would go to prison, with D choosing to go there to pursue the hidden stage.
- Over the whole match robbery was used three times and stones not once.

### Differences from our version
- Show: 30 × 30 board and 9 players; ours: 14 × 14 and 2–4.
- Show: one public queue of 96 tiles, with a coupon to pick among the next five; ours: a private hand of 3 random pieces from 17 shapes.
- Show: start from a 1 × 1 base tile and one coupon; ours: a fixed start square, no coupons.
- Show: no edge contact with opponents; our page asks only for edge contact with your own squares.
- Show: coupon, piece, stone and robbery squares, triggered by surrounding them; ours: Encore and Leap squares, triggered by covering them.
- Show: scored by the largest square, in pieces; ours: 10 points per owned square.
- Show: 9 rounds, then optional 1 × 1 purchases; ours: until nobody can place or 40 turns each.
- Show: 2-minute limit, unplaceable tiles discarded; ours: no limit, a stuck player is skipped.

### Gaps
- The shapes of the 96 tiles and the positions and counts of the special squares are not in the text.
- "Surround on four sides" and "may not be placed against a stone" are read from unclear translation.
- It is unclear whether the bought 1 × 1 tile had to join one's own territory.
- Piece counts do not fully add up for D: D started on 3, bought a 1 × 1 tile and took the 4 × 4 penalty, yet is listed with 2.
- The guide's day summary names E, H and I as match winners; its results table and course give E, F and I.
