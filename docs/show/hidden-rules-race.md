## Season 2 (Death Room), Day 2: main match
- **Name in the source:** Unknown (the rule text also calls it Union)
- **Players:** 12, each for themselves
- **Stakes:** 1st place 3 pieces, 2nd to 5th 1 piece each. Prize mission: exactly one player eliminated during the match earns 100 million won. Players could also lose pieces, and be eliminated, through a hidden penalty.

### Rules on the show
**Open rules**
- A board of numbered squares in six colours (red, yellow, blue, green, purple, white), with grey starting areas around it and one goal square.
- Each player gets a cube with six coloured faces; all cubes are laid out the same way.
- Players choose a starting area (conflicts settled by agreement). Turn order runs from the lowest starting-area number and never changes, even if a piece is later sent to another area.
- On a turn, a player rolls their cube one square up, down, left or right. If another piece is there, it is rolled on in the same direction.
- A round is one turn each. The game ends as soon as any piece reaches the goal: that player is first. The others rank by the number of the square they stand on, highest first.
- A practice game of three rounds is played first with the same rules on a different board (the players are not told it differs), then there is one hour of strategy time on the real board.

**Hidden rules** (the dealer applies them without explaining them; one more is announced at the end of each round, in order)
1. If a piece's top face is white, or it reaches a white square, it takes one banishment mark and goes back to a starting area of its owner's choice. There is no other way back to a starting area.
2. The first and second players to collect 3 banishment marks must return 3 pieces (someone without enough pieces is eliminated).
3. A pushed piece triggers no further rules, except banishment.
4. If none of the nine squares around and under a piece (3×3, including pieces standing just off the board) has the colour of its top face, the top colour must be changed.
5. It is changed to one of the colours found in those nine squares.
6. If at least three of the nine, counting the piece's own top, share the top colour, the player moves one other piece of that colour (nothing happens if there is none).
7. If that colour is yellow, any other piece may be chosen.
8. If three of the top colour, counting the piece itself, form a straight line (diagonals work too), the player takes one more turn. Other triggered rules are resolved first, and the extra turn happens only if the line still stands.
- Order of resolution: each chain is followed to the end (depth first); a moved piece's own triggers come before the mover's extra turn.
- Reaching the goal beats everything, including banishment: the game stops at once.

### How it went
- Practice: players found the white banishment, the 3-mark penalty (A reached three marks and learned it would cost 3 pieces) and the forced colour change, and guessed at the movement of other pieces.
- Before the match the living-quarters players worked as a group of seven and the prisoners as a group of five, with some crossing over. B suggested letting two allies with 1 piece finish first and second. No group wanted to push for an elimination hard, partly because fewer survivors means fewer living-quarters places.
- Starts: three 4-piece players (B, C, D) took the areas farthest from the goal; E and F took areas with a short route.
- Round 1: no extra turns. Several guesses about the triggers proved too loose (two yellows, or an L shape, did nothing). Rule 1 revealed.
- Round 2: B bounced between two blue squares, earning a blue move of another piece every round and pushing allies along. G got a red move and chose to banish the leader A rather than just push A back. A's group switched to winning with H. Rule 2 revealed.
- Round 3: C was banished (a mistake made in an earlier relocation). B repeated the blue trick. E came within a few squares of the goal; D declined to attack. F considered selling a move to I (one of the living group, on 1 piece), but I declined, unsure whether a white top at the goal would count; the dealer refused to say. The prisoners blocked A and advanced E. H then chained extra turns, banishing F and E and moving I forward; J had been banished earlier in the round. Rule 3 revealed.
- Round 4: D's yellow move set H up near the goal, but H's piece now covered a square in a way that gave G a green move. G banished H, turning down H's offer of a piece. The group switched to I. With the banishment rule now public, the dealer confirmed the goal comes first. K moved I forward, I used a blue move on A, and A used the resulting blue move to push I onto the goal.

### Differences from our version
- 12 players taking turns; ours seats 4-8 and everyone moves at once.
- Rolling cubes whose top colour matters, with pushing; ours has plain pieces moved by a direction.
- The secrets are triggers (banishment, colour change, moving others, extra turns); ours gives each of five square colours one movement rule.
- Six colours, numbered squares, grey start areas; ours is a 7×7 five-colour board with a centre goal.
- One rule revealed after every round; ours reveals a rule after rounds 6, 12 and 18, with a 30-round cap.
- Show ranking by square number; ours by distance to the goal.
- Piece penalties, deal-making, a practice game and strategy time are absent from ours.
- Our How to play says four players behind the winner got one piece each for the highest squares; the source agrees.

## Season 2 (Death Room), game-rules page
Source: the game-rules page has no block for this game; these points come from folded text, footnotes and move logs of the Day 2 page.

### Rules added
- The squares of the real board are numbered up to at least 132; the winning move went from square 131 onto the goal. On the practice board the goal was entered from square 100.
- Start positions carry low numbers: the twelve starting picks of the real game lay between 1 and 20, and banished players were later put back on such numbers.
- Whenever the extra-turn condition (a line of three) holds, the condition for moving another piece (three of the colour nearby) holds too, so the other piece is moved first and the extra turn follows if the line remains.

### Corrections to the day record
- Gap "the boards' layout and size are only in images": only partly so. The layout is in images, but the logs show that the real board has well over 100 numbered squares, far more than the 7 x 7 board of our version.

### Gaps
- Where the goal sits on the board is still not said in the text.
- The exact number of squares and of starting positions is not stated.
- No round limit is mentioned.
