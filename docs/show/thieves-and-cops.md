## Season 2 (Death Room), Day 1: main match
- **Name in the source:** Corrupt Police (also rendered Corruption Police)
- **Players:** 14. Three police teams of four (red, green, blue) and two thieves. Two of the twelve officers were secretly corrupt, in two different teams. By team: red A, B, C, D (no corrupt officer); green E, F (corrupt), G, H; blue I, J, K, L (corrupt); thieves M and N.
- **Stakes:** game pieces by role (below). The top half of the piece ranking went to the living quarters, the rest to prison. Prize mission: finish by round 10 for 100 million won, 20 million less for each later round (nothing at round 15).

### Rules on the show
**Roles**
- Drawn by lot. Who the thieves are is made public; the corrupt officers learn each other.
- The thieves know who the corrupt officers are.
- Each police team and the thieves play from separate, isolated rooms.
- 30 minutes of strategy time before the start.

**Board and setup**
- A board of numbered stations modelled on the Seoul subway map.
- 28 pieces are hidden on stations; only the thieves are shown where.
- Officers place their markers first, and these are shown to everyone. Several markers may share a station.
- The thieves then place themselves, not on a piece station. A thief's position stays secret until arrest.

**Rounds** (up to 15; thieves first, then police)
- Thief turn (3 minutes): move up to 2 stations. Passing through or stopping on a piece station collects the piece. The thieves' new positions are then sent to the corrupt officers.
- Police turn (5 minutes per team): the teams act one after another, and the starting order rotates each round. All police actions are visible live on a monitor.
- Each officer takes two actions (as the move log shows), each on their own station or one station away. Team members may act in any order, and an officer may use the same action twice.
- **Investigate:** tells whether a thief has passed through that station at least once ("clue" or "no clue"). Only the acting officer sees the answer, tells the team aloud and may lie. They then choose whether to share it; a shared result is shown to all teams.
- **Arrest:** if a thief is on that station, they are arrested at once. The outcome is public. A corrupt officer's arrest always shows as failed.
- An arrested thief loses their pieces and is taken to a separate room.
- After rounds 5 and 10 the number of pieces the thieves hold is announced.
- Free talk outside one's own room was allowed up to the start of round 14 (wording unclear).

**End**
- The thieves hold 12 pieces together, or both thieves are arrested, or round 15 ends.
- Pieces lie far enough apart that the thieves' total can never pass 13.

**Final vote**
- Inside each police team, everyone secretly writes the name of the teammate they think is corrupt, or votes "none".
- The most-voted name is accused. If "none" leads, nobody is accused; there is no reward for being right about that.

**Pieces awarded**
- Honest officers: 1 piece each for a successful thief arrest and 1 for unmasking a corrupt officer (a team could earn at most 3 per honest officer).
- Thieves pay half of the pieces they collected to the corrupt officers who were not unmasked; the surviving thieves split the rest.
- If both corrupt officers are unmasked, nothing is paid to them.
- Example: 12 pieces, two free thieves and two hidden corrupt officers gives 3 pieces to each of the four.

### How it went
- Before the draw, C had a pact with M and L to share information if any of them drew a special role. C, an honest officer on a team with no corrupt member, agreed with the thieves that red would not catch them and would act suspiciously, in return for places in the living quarters for three of the four red players.
- Separately, I had a pre-draw pact with N and learned from N that blue held a corrupt officer.
- The thieves started near L and F, who from the first round searched along the thieves' routes themselves to keep others from finding traces; F also steered green elsewhere.
- Rounds 1-3: A attempted arrests early and C seemed to retrace steps, so green and blue took red for the corrupt team and stopped trusting it. Green and blue divided the search between them. In round 3, I told blue it had a corrupt officer; all denied it.
- L passed that news to M.
- Round 5: the thieves held 6 pieces. Others accused red of sabotage.
- Round 6: E found the first clue and kept it off the shared screen to keep red uninformed, then told blue in person. F found one too and made an arrest attempt that failed. L also reported a clue; the net around M tightened.
- Round 7: green and blue tried several arrests around M. M falsely led blue to believe J was the corrupt officer.
- Round 8: M moved right onto the station of A, counting on the last team in the order being unable to pass information on in time. A moved away without finding M.
- Rounds 9-10: the thieves reached 10 pieces. M took a risky route towards the next piece, and green searched the wrong branch.
- Round 11: each thief collected one more piece, 12 in all, and the game ended.

### Result
- Thieves won in round 11; nobody was arrested.
- Red vote: one vote against C, three "none": nobody accused (correct, red had no corrupt officer).
- Green vote: three of four named E (honest); F escaped.
- Blue vote: K received two votes (from J and L), L one (from I), K voted "none": K accused (honest); L escaped.
- M, N, F and L gained 3 pieces each (4 each); the other ten stayed on 1.
- Prize fund: 80 million won.
- The four winners had to fill three more living-quarters places from the ten players tied on 1 piece. F objected to giving all three to red. The compromise: C and D, plus one more picked by a game of chance; E won it and passed the place to K.
- Prison: A, B, E, G, H, I, J.

### Differences from our version
- 14 players with three police teams and two corrupt officers; ours seats 4-8 (you and 3 bots) with one corrupt officer and no teams.
- Thieves move first, up to 2 stations; police then act team by team. Ours moves everyone at once, one stop.
- Police investigate or arrest on their own or a neighbouring station; ours catches by meeting or passing a thief.
- Show clues can be lied about and withheld; ours has public sightings after rounds 2, 4, 6, 8 and 10 and shows where a piece is taken. The show announced only piece totals after rounds 5 and 10.
- Large subway board, 28 pieces, goal 12, up to 15 rounds; ours 17 stations, 7 pieces, goal 5, 12 rounds.
- Piece positions were shown to the thieves only; ours also shows them to the corrupt officer.
- Corrupt arrests are attempted and shown as failures; in ours the corrupt officer simply never catches.
- Vote inside each team with a "none" option; in ours every officer names one other officer.
- Rewards in pieces with a thief-to-corrupt payment; ours gives points.

### Gaps
- One reward condition is garbled: it seems to deny honest officers the thief-arrest piece in some case involving corrupt officers; the case is unclear.
- Not clear whether the arrest reward goes only to the arresting officer's team or to all honest officers.
- The exact limits on talking between teams up to round 14 are unclear.
- The two-actions-per-officer count is read from the move log; the rule sentence itself is garbled.
- Parts of the round 10 moves were not shown.

## Season 2 (Death Room), game-rules page
Source: the game-rules page has no block for this game; these points come from folded text, footnotes and the move log of the Day 1 page.

### Rules added
- **Piece stations** (28): 1, 5, 9, 14, 18, 22, 29, 41, 45, 50, 54, 60, 64, 73, 77, 93, 97, 109, 113, 122, 128, 138, 142, 148, 151, 155, 162, 171. Station numbers in the log go up to at least 172.
- A footnote explains that the pieces are spaced more than three stations apart, which limits what can be taken in a round and keeps the thieves' total at 13 or less.
- **Rooms:** thieves in the dealer room; red team in the warehouse, blue team in the meeting room, green team in the kitchen.
- The log confirms two actions per officer, and the same kind may be taken twice (two arrest attempts in one turn occur). One officer's turn is recorded as timed out, with no action, and one officer gave up the second action.

### Corrections to the day record

### Gaps
