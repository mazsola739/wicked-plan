# Either Or on the show

How this game was played on The Devil's Plan. Our version: [rules page](../games/either-or.md).
Recorded from the NamuWiki episode guides (machine-translated), in our own words. Contestants
are Player A, B …; letters are assigned separately in each section. **Gaps** lists what the source
leaves open. Index: [On the show](README.md).

## Season 3 (Dead Again), Day 1: main match
- **Name in the source:** Either/Or (first main match)
- **Players:** 16, each for themselves; temporary teams of three in one mini-game, plus open alliances
- **Stakes:** ranks 1-6 to the living quarters, 7-14 to the prison, 15-16 eliminated; the winner got the Alliance Token (moves one prison-bound player to the living quarters); some choices paid pieces, one could add a prize-money token

### Rules on the show
- 10 two-option choices and 4 mini-games, revealed one at a time in the order: choice 1, mini-game 1 (Count the Cubes), choice 2, mini-game 2 (Count the Rectangles), choice 3, choice 4, choice 5, mini-game 3 (Lay the Grass) with choices 6-8 inside it, choice 9, choice 10, mini-game 4 (Memory Poker).
- Answers were entered on terminals. Choices and mini-games gave or took victory points; the totals of all players were shown whenever they changed. The mini-games have their own records.
- **Choice 1:** "performance bonus" (1 piece at the end if you end up in the living quarters) or "insurance" (2 pieces if you end up in the prison).
- **Choice 2** (before mini-game 2): play it in a team of three or alone.
- **Choice 3** (depends on mini-game 2):
  - Each trio names a representative.
  - Winning trio, by majority inside the team: representative +5 and the two others +1 each, or representative +1 and the others +3 each.
  - Losing trio, decided by its representative: representative -3, or the two others -2 each.
  - Solo players: "challenge" (an extra match; win 3 pieces, or lose 3 points) or "safety" (-1 point). If only one player challenged, that player would win without a match.
- **Choice 4:** "public interest" (if 9 or more picked it, a prize-money token) or "personal interest" (+5 points for each who picked it).
- **Choice 5:** +5 points, or an advance look at choices 6, 7 and 8.
- **Choice 6:** "attack" (a placed grass tile scores double; failing to place costs 4 points) or "defence" (may trim up to 2 squares off your tile).
- **Choice 7:** "win-win" (pair up; bonus points equal to the number of square edges where the two tiles touch, split by agreement) or "check" (+5 if 9 or fewer players, you included, place their tile).
- **Choice 8:** place one street tree, or place one +1 and one -1 single-square manhole.
- **Choice 9:** if 8 pieces were paid in total, only the last-placed player would be eliminated; otherwise the bottom two. Paying was optional.
- **Choice 10** sets the form of mini-game 4: "nomination" (needs 8 or more votes) or "ladder" (needs 9 or more).
- Ties in rank were broken by the priority order from mini-game 1. From mini-game 4 on, points no longer mattered, only rank positions.

### How it went
- Choice 1: 9 took the performance bonus, 7 the insurance.
- Mini-game 1: Player A solved it first, Player B second.
- Choice 2: three trios formed and seven played solo. Player C joined A and B; Player D turned down offers and played alone.
- Mini-game 2: D won first; the A-B-C trio and solo players E, F and G were the other winners.
- Choice 3: C was the winning trio's representative; A and B outvoted C's wish for the representative-heavy split. Both losing trios made their representative pay. Among the solo players only D and G chose the challenge; D won it (3 pieces) and G lost 3 points. The other five took -1.
- Choice 4: C tried to rally everyone to the public side and F was the first to commit to it, while players low in points leaned to the personal one; C later switched to personal. Player H told others they had picked public interest but had picked personal. The vote ended 8-8: no token.
- Choice 5: only F and Player I took the preview (14 took the points). F shared it with a large alliance formed around the public-interest voters; I shared it late and with few.
- Choices 6-8: 5 attacked and 11 defended; 10 chose win-win and 6 check; 3 chose the tree and 13 the manholes.
- Mini-game 3: the alliance set out to block I, who picked first. Most players, including some who were behind I in tie order, took the same large tile, and the late ones in line found no room. 9 players placed, 7 did not.
- Points after mini-game 3: D 34, Player J 30, A 29, E 29, B 21, Player K 20, H 20, G 18, Player L 11, C 11, F 6, Player M 5, Player N 5, Player O 5, Player P 2, I 1.
- Choice 9: no pieces were paid. I asked for help, but the total needed D's pieces and D had already refused.
- Choice 10: the ladder won, 10 votes to 6.
- Mini-game 4: I and P were the bottom two. I lost once and was out. O, pushed down to 15th by P's early win, climbed to 11th with a run of wins and was stopped by C. P fell back to 15th and was out.

### Differences from our version
- Ours has 14 rounds (10 two-way questions and 4 surprise rounds); the show had 10 choices plus 4 separate skill mini-games.
- Our questions pay by majority or minority. On the show nearly every choice had its own special effect (pieces, points, team splits, rule changes for the next mini-game, elimination rules); only a few depended on the vote count.
- Lowest Unique, Share or Grab, Point the Finger and Lone Wolf did not appear.
- Ours seats 3-8 and ranks by total points; the show had 16 players, tie order from a mini-game, and final places decided by a duel ladder.
- No teams, pieces, prize token or elimination in ours.

### Gaps
- What the extra challenge match of choice 3 was is not told.
- The result of choice 9 was not broadcast; only that no pieces were paid.
- The passage on how the Alliance Token was handed over is garbled; the result table clearly shows C receiving it, and says F chose to go to prison without it.
- Whether team members other than the representative had any say in the losing-team choice is not clear.
