## Season 1, Day 3: main match
- **Name in the source:** Secret Number
- **Players:** 11, each for themselves; temporary alliances formed
- **Stakes:** pieces by final score; the lowest rank loses extra pieces; separately, a side bet on who would be imprisoned that day paid 1 piece for a correct guess

### Rules on the show
- Every player is given a different secret number from 1 to 100. Nobody is told any number, not even their own.
- Clues come only from contacts. There are two contact rooms; two players enter one and each hands in one contact ticket of the same type.
- Every player starts with one set of four tickets and can buy one more set for 1 piece during the game. A ticket was also handed from one player to another.
- Ticket types, each applied to the two players' numbers:
  - **Plus:** their sum. Extreme sums are blurred: a sum of 20 or less is reported only as "3 to 20", a sum of 180 or more only as "180 to 199".
  - **Multiply:** the last digit of their product (33 and 22 give 726, so 6).
  - **Divide:** the whole-number quotient of the larger by the smaller; the remainder is dropped.
  - **Zero:** how many zeros appear in the numbers lying between the two, the two themselves excluded (44 and 88 give 4: 50, 60, 70, 80).
- The game lasts 2 hours. At the end each player fills in an answer sheet with the numbers of all players, their own included.
- Scoring: own number +5 if right, -5 if wrong; each other player's number +1 if right, -1 if wrong; a blank costs nothing; +5 bonus for getting every player right; -1 for each other player who guessed your number correctly.
- Pieces by final score: 16 or more +3; 11 to 15 +2; 6 to 10 +1; exactly 5 no change; 0 to 4 -1; -1 or less -2. The lowest rank loses 3 more. If all players tie, each loses 1.
- Side bet before the match: each player names one person they expect to be imprisoned that day. A hit pays 1 piece. No base stake and no extra stake this time.

### How it went
- Before the match Players A, B and C, seen by others as a strong group, planned to win over Players D and E. Players F, G and H wanted a bloc of their own and to keep A, B and C in check. Pieces at the start: A 3, B 3, C 3, D 2, E 1, F 2, G 2, H 2, I 1, J 1, K 1.
- The common plan was an alliance of three: all contacts among them, one bought ticket set, and all three numbers can be solved.
- J expected a place with A and K, but A took C instead. A bought the extra set, and A, C and K were the first to solve their numbers (A 59, C 7, K 34).
- J joined F, G and H, who planned a method for four; I, left alone, joined too, making a bloc of five. H bought an extra set, which served to work out I's number.
- B, D and E formed the third group. B and E used all four tickets between them. B then bought an extra set; a sum worked out wrongly caused confusion, which D and E cleared up with a Divide contact.
- A gave J a spare Zero ticket as compensation.
- With about 20 minutes left, H saw B put an arm round C and speak to C, and concluded that B had leaked numbers. The five-player bloc told D and E that B had betrayed them. E doubted it, but D gave F the numbers of B, D and E, and F confirmed them with a contact with D.
- In fact B had leaked nothing; B had only warned C about a suspected spy (K, who was not one).
- Answer sheets went in with about 3 minutes left. F, G, H and J entered the numbers of one another, of I, of B and of E, but not D's. I did not enter E's number.
- Contact results shown: A-C Plus 66, Multiply 3, Divide 8; A-K Plus 93, Multiply 6, Divide 1; B-E Plus 138, Multiply 6, Divide 1, Zero 3; D-E Divide 7; F-G Plus 68, Multiply 2, Divide 1, Zero 2; H-J Plus 110, Multiply 4, Divide 5, Zero 8.
- Numbers: B 54, D 11, E 84, F 42, G 26, H 94, J 16, plus A, C and K above; I's was not shown.

### Result
- Scores: F, G, H, J 7; I 6; A, C, D, K 5; E 1; B 0.
- Piece change from the match, ticket purchases included: F, G, I, J +1; C, D, H, K 0; A -1; E -1 (to 0); B -3 (to 0).
- B and E were eliminated.
- H (despite sharing the top score) and K went to prison. C, D and I had bet on H and each gained 1 piece.
- Pieces after the bet: C 4; D, F, G, I 3; A, H, J 2; K 1; B, E 0.

### Differences from our version
- Show: you do not know your own number and must deduce it; ours tells you yours.
- Show: 11 players in free conversation for 2 hours; ours 4 to 8 seats over 5 fixed rounds.
- Show: contact tickets give facts about two numbers combined (sum, product digit, quotient, zeros) to both players; ours swaps one clue about each other's number (parity, threshold, digits, divisibility by 3).
- Show: one answer sheet at the end; ours has a guess in every round, with immediate effect, public exposure and a higher/lower hint on a miss.
- Show: scoring in single points (+5/-5 own, +1/-1 others, -1 per player who found you, +5 all-correct bonus); ours +50, -30, -5, and +40 for staying hidden.
- Show: limited tickets, an extra set bought with a piece, tickets transferable; ours has neither tickets nor purchases.
- Show: pieces, elimination and prison; ours only ranks by score.

Source: the general rules of the Season 1 game-rules page; it has no section on this game itself.

### Rules added
- Season-wide prison rule: after a main match the two players with the fewest pieces are imprisoned. If one player is alone at the bottom and several tie for the next lowest count, the lowest goes for certain and one of the tied players is picked to go too.

### Corrections to the day record
- Day 3 gap "how the prisoners were chosen": the Day 3 piece counts fit this rule (one player alone on 1 piece, the other prisoner among those tied on 2).

### Gaps
- Neither page says who picked the second prisoner on Day 3; elsewhere the rules page gives that choice to the player with the most pieces.
- The rules page adds nothing on the contact tickets, scoring or last-place tie of this game.
