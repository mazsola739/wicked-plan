## Season 1, Day 5: hidden stage (secret room)
- **Name in the source:** Blind Omok (machine translation also "Blind Concave").
- **Players:** one challenger, Player A, against a non-player opponent in a black robe.
- **Stakes:** a loss eliminated the challenger. The same challenge, won the next day, paid pieces.

### Rules on the show
- Five in a row on a Go-style board. The challenger played black and moved first; the opponent played white.
- The challenge could be attempted once a day.
- The game relies on memorising the board.

### How it went
- Two prisoners opened the way to the secret room; A chose to go first, in the evening.
- A was confident at gomoku but worried about memory. When picking stones, A gathered several of a colour in advance to save time and concentration.
- The record runs to 12 turns.

### Result
- A lost and was eliminated at once (5th of the season), without a chance to say goodbye. The living quarters learned of it from a bell and the lights going out early.

### Differences from our version
- Show: failure means elimination, success pays pieces; ours: points.
- Show: one attempt per day; ours: no limit.
- The other points of our page (9 × 9, only the newest tile in colour, two peeks, the bot's memory) cannot be compared from this page.

### Gaps
- Board size, how stones are hidden or coloured, timers and the exact win condition are not on this page; it refers to a separate game-rules page for the hidden stage.
- The move record is graphical: no coordinates and no winning line in the text.

## Season 1, Day 6: hidden stage (secret room)
- **Name in the source:** Blind Omok (machine translation also "Blind Concave").
- **Players:** one challenger, Player A, against a non-player opponent.
- **Stakes:** pieces for a win (A went from 3 to 13). The previous day's challenger, Player B, had lost and been eliminated.

### Rules on the show
- Five in a row. A played black; the opponent played white.
- The challenge could be attempted once a day.

### How it went
- A went in at about 8 in the morning.
- A used a fixed personal pattern of stone colours as a memory aid (apparently every fourth stone in yellow).
- The opponent went only for its own line and did not block A's.
- A completed five in a row with the 19th stone of the game (turn 10).
- A wondered at the easy finish but was sure of having made no mistake; tearful and thinking of B, A left saying B had been avenged on whoever set the game.

### Result
- A won: 3 pieces to 13 (+10).
- Back in the living quarters A gave a short account of the secret room.

### Differences from our version
- Show: a win pays pieces and a loss eliminates; ours: points.
- Show: one attempt per day; ours: no limit.
- The show's player could apparently choose stone colours, which suggests colour did not mark the owner; on our page only the newest tile shows its colour.

### Gaps
- Board size, the visibility rules and timers are not on this page.
- The translation of A's colour pattern is garbled.
- The move record is graphical: no coordinates in the text.

## Season 1, game-rules page
Source: the "hidden stage" part of the Season 1 game-rules page; it covers the Day 5 and Day 6 secret-room matches.

### Rules added
- **Access:** the secret room is reached from the prison, through a locked safe behind a wall outlet and a secret passage. One prison inmate per day may challenge.
- **Stake:** the challenger hands in all of their pieces. A win returns them plus 10 more. A loss forfeits them all and eliminates the challenger.
- **Format:** a single game of five in a row with Go stones, against a non-player opponent. The challenger is the black side and moves first; the opponent is the white side and moves second.
- **What makes it blind:** the stones come in assorted colours (the page lists red, orange, yellow, green, blue, purple, pink and others, the underside being the exception), so a stone's colour does not show whose it is. The challenger picks the stone for each own move, and the opponent answers with a stone of that same colour.
- **Line rules:** free-style gomoku, as the page names it (see Gaps for the forbidden moves).
- **Fouls:** cheating or trickery loses the game at once. Examples given as not allowed: marking your own hand as a record, playing off the line on purpose to read the opponent's reaction, keeping stones off the board as a memory aid, shouting information to people outside the prison.
- **Allowed memory aid:** choosing colours on purpose, for instance in a pattern by turn order or for the outer moves.
- **Opponent:** the person at the board only places the stones; the moves come from a gomoku program. According to a producer interview the page cites, it was set to the easiest level (about a 50% win rate).

### Corrections to the day record
- Day 5 and Day 6, stakes: not only "a loss eliminates, a win pays pieces". All pieces are put at stake, and a win pays exactly 10 on top of their return (this matches the Day 6 result, 3 to 13).
- Day 5 and Day 6, "once a day": the limit is one inmate per day, and only an inmate can challenge.
- Day 6: it is confirmed (not just apparent) that the challenger chose the stone colours and that colour did not mark the owner.
- Our differences, now comparable: on the show every stone has a visible but meaningless colour, picked by the challenger and copied by the opponent (ours: older tiles face down, only the newest shows its owner's colour); no peek is mentioned (ours: two); the show's opponent was an easy-level gomoku program (ours: a bot with imperfect memory).

### Gaps
- Board size and timers are not given.
- What is on the underside of the stones is not said.
- The note on forbidden moves is garbled: the game is called free-rule (normally no restrictions, overlines included), but the translated aside reads as if double threes, double fours or lines of six or more were not allowed.
- Whether a draw is possible, and what it would do to the stake, is not said.
